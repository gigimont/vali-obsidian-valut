## Where Things Stand

- Significant acceleration in fieldwork — 4 SME owner interviews completed in Poland alone this week, plus Stefan Weiler (Filigran, Germany)
- Core thesis continues to hold and sharpen: knowledge management for SMEs is a real, unaddressed problem
- Competitors exist in the broader space but no one specifically targets this niche
- Framing sharpening further: solution sits within "knowledge management" broadly, but specifically around making expert knowledge scalable inside SMEs
- Most SME knowledge lives with founders and senior employees — no formal processes
- Concrete example surfaced: one company doubled salary to retain a field expert rather than lose their knowledge — highlights the cost of expertise loss

---

## Key Conversations This Week

### 4 Polish SME owners (via Wolf's network)
- 3 companies on succession cliff: 2 have successors, 1 selling
- 1 solo entrepreneur / service middleman
- Key insight confirmed across all: **human relationships drive SME business operations** — trust between people is the core competitive advantage, everything else (processes, systems) can be copied or learned
- Identified a spectrum of digitalization levels:
  - High end: Polish-American entrepreneur with 30-40 ERP processes
  - Low end: 5-6 employee real estate companies (entirely relationship-dependent)

### [[Interview 012 — Stefan Weiler (Filigran, Germany)]]
- Wolf's uncle, CEO of 3rd-generation steel manufacturer (~110 employees)
- 77-year-old company with zero knowledge preservation plan
- All Italian patterns confirmed in German manufacturing context
- 2-4 year succession decision window — strong pilot candidate

- Large enough to spend on solutions
- Sweet spot between over-digitized (like Lynka at €40-50M) and too small (micro-businesses under €2M)
