## ⚠️ Significance of This Interview

**Lorenz is the most senior PE professional in the dataset**, operating at a significantly larger scale (€50-150M per deal) than previous M&A contacts. He gave the first constructive challenge to the M&A-first GTM strategy: "I'm struggling to imagine why you'd limit to M&A. The value of knowledge is event-driven — it can happen at any time." This is not a rejection of the product — it's a reframing that EXPANDS the market. He also validated "information vulnerability mapping" as immediately useful to him as a board member.

## What We Learned

### Hard vs soft knowledge (investor's framing)
- **Hard knowledge** (processes, documentation, customer history): "You'll find it somewhere again." Recoverable even if poorly documented.
- **Soft knowledge** (relationships with key decision makers, business judgment, gut feeling, intuition from 25-30 years): "Way more important than hard knowledge." Impossible to write down traditionally. This is what kills deals and derails transitions.
- **Product implication:** Our product needs to explicitly target the SOFT layer, not just the hard layer. The hard layer is table stakes; the soft layer is where the real value is. This aligns with the "judgment framework capture" framing from [[Interview 011 — Dairy Chemicals Commercial (Italy)]].

### Red flags that investors watch for (due diligence lens)
- **Sales org dependent on founder CEO:** Biggest red flag. If revenue requires the founder's personal relationships, the company is fragile.
- **Technology/innovation dependent on founder:** If the founder drives product development and the product needs continuous innovation, departure = relevance loss.
- **Brand identity concentrated in founder:** Okio example — luxury lighting. Founder left, new team couldn't replicate his design vision, company lost market relevance. "Brand identity is a very difficult piece of knowledge to encapsulate."
- **What's NOT a red flag:** Strategic and operational involvement by the CEO — "that's rather easy to transfer."
- **Product implication:** Our "vulnerability mapping" deliverable should map exactly these dimensions: sales dependency, innovation dependency, brand dependency, operational dependency. Give investors a red/yellow/green dashboard.

### The M&A-only GTM challenge (STRATEGIC — may require pivot)
- Lorenz explicitly questioned limiting to M&A: "Doesn't make sense for you to focus only on M&A"
- His reasoning: knowledge vulnerability is EVENT-DRIVEN, not M&A-driven
- Events that trigger knowledge loss: someone resigns, someone retires, restructuring, hiring/firing, process changes
- "M&A is a huge event. But it can happen at any time."
- He envisions an ALWAYS-ON tool: "I would expect to have a tool that qualifies information that my chief marketing officer has, for whatever reason."
- **Product implication:** The M&A angle is valid but may be too narrow. The broader framing is: "continuous knowledge vulnerability monitoring" — a tool that a board member or CEO uses ongoing, not just during transactions. The M&A moment is when the tool becomes CRITICAL, but its value accrues daily.
- **Cross-reference:** This contradicts our May 9 standup decision to go M&A-first. But it doesn't invalidate it — it suggests M&A is the ENTRY POINT (highest urgency, clearest budget) while the ongoing monitoring is the RETENTION play. Sell with M&A, retain with always-on.

### Information vulnerability mapping (the concept that landed)
- When Wolf described the product as mapping where knowledge is concentrated and what breaks when someone leaves, Lorenz engaged immediately
- Use cases he saw:
  - Board member: "qualifying what the CEO tells me" — triangulating information
  - Hiring/firing: "how much knowledge are you gonna be losing?"
  - Decision-making: "what information is lacking from a certain decision?"
  - CEO departure: "being able to visualize and contextualize what information is where"
- "That would be useful to me as an investor. It would also be valuable to the CEO."
- **Product implication:** "Information vulnerability map" or "knowledge concentration dashboard" could be a standalone deliverable name. It's concrete, it's visual, and Lorenz understood it immediately.

### Due diligence at €50-150M scale
- 6-week advisor engagement: financials, legal, commercial, customer interviews
- Output: green/yellow/red flag system
- Advisors are insured (errors covered by insurance) — this is why funds outsource
- Billing: hourly, proportional to company size
- For smaller add-on acquisitions (~€5-20M): just send one M&A person for 1-2 weeks, much less formal
- **Product implication for pricing:** At €50-150M deal size, EMH's DD budget is in the hundreds of thousands. Our €15-25K fee is a rounding error. For smaller add-on acquisitions (our ICP size), the DD budget is tighter — but that's where our product fills the gap because they do LESS due diligence, not more.

### Post-acquisition value creation (where AI fits and doesn't)
- Typical 5-year value creation plan: add-on acquisitions, geographic expansion, product development
- Digitalization is NOT usually part of the value creation thesis — "most topics aren't really based on that"
- Exception: marketing/IT services where AI directly disrupts service delivery (Liganova example: design work went from 10-20 hours to 2 minutes with AI)
- "Hire a CTO, hire someone who knows how to do this. I invest, I put money in. That's it."
- **Product implication:** Don't position to PE funds as a digitalization tool. Position as a RISK REDUCTION tool and a KNOWLEDGE CONTINUITY tool. They don't care about making companies digital; they care about protecting their investment.

### Guarantee and indemnity structures (new knowledge)
- Sellers guarantee that certain things won't happen (key customer departure, environmental issues, legal battles)
- Can go as far as: "if the customer leaves, reimburse half the purchase price"
- Change of control clauses in customer contracts = huge risk factor
- **Product implication:** Our knowledge extraction can REDUCE the need for aggressive indemnity clauses — if the buyer has documented the key relationships and knowledge, they're less dependent on the seller staying. This reduces both parties' risk and could directly affect deal structure.

### Celonis comparison — validated with caveats
- "Their use case stops making sense below €250M revenue. The complexity isn't high enough."
- EMH has only one portfolio company large enough for Celonis
- The "Celonis for analog companies" framing works conceptually but Lorenz notes the scale mismatch
- **Product implication:** The Celonis comparison is useful for explaining what we do, but be careful not to position as "small Celonis" — position as "the layer Celonis can't reach" (tacit knowledge, not digital process logs).
