## Key Quotes

> "Lack of documented processes and concentration of operational knowledge in a few key employees is one of the most common sources of hidden friction in mid-market M&A transactions."

> "It's rarely a standalone deal breaker, but it really often creates an accumulation of smaller red/orange flags that reduce buyer confidence in proceeding further or even closing the deal."

> "The impact is concentrated in prolonged due diligence timelines, increased perceived operational risk, and pressure on valuation and deal structure."

> "Buyers start to ask: what happens if the current CEO or COO leaves? Can the business actually operate independently from the founder?"

> "Usually the answers rely primarily on management explanations rather than documented operational reality."

> "The retained part of the price is typically 10, maybe up to 20%. Any higher level would be quite difficult to find common ground with the seller."

> "Advisory agreements — I was quite shocked in most cases how generous the buyer could be. Like 20K euro monthly plus incentive, or 30, 40K per month."

> "90% of the risks, if the buyer and seller are motivated to make a deal, there is a solution. One way or another."

> "The Polish entrepreneurs in their early 50s are now thinking about making a hard stop. The age is getting younger."

## What We Learned

### Knowledge gap = accumulated red flags (SHARPENS Pattern 17)
- Undocumented knowledge is "rarely a standalone deal breaker" but creates an ACCUMULATION of smaller issues
- These accumulate across multiple DD streams: commercial, financial, legal
- Impact: prolonged DD timelines, additional Q&A rounds, management sessions, even workshops
- Buyers hire additional commercial DD teams (more cost for everyone)
- Result: earn-outs, deferred payments, escrow mechanisms, reduced offers
- **Sharpened pitch:** Don't say "undocumented knowledge kills deals." Say "undocumented knowledge creates a cascade of friction that erodes price, extends timelines, and adds deal structure complexity."

### Deal structure costs are now quantifiable

| Mechanism | Typical range | Triggered by knowledge gaps? |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Earn-out / deferred payment | 10-20% of deal price | Yes — lower documentation = higher deferred % |
| Escrow | Up to 10% of deal price | Yes — specific risks held in escrow |
| Advisory agreement (founder retention) | €20-40K/month for 12-24 months | Yes — knowledge in founder's head = must retain |
| R&W Insurance | 1-3% of enterprise value | Partially — reduces need to chase seller for claims |
| Extended DD | Additional advisor team cost | Yes — management explanations instead of documented reality |

**Product ROI case at a €20M deal:**
- Earn-out reduction of 5% = €1M saved
- Advisory agreement compression (24 months → 6 months) at €30K/month = €540K saved
- Total potential saving: €1M+ against our fee of €15-25K. 40-60x ROI.

### DD takes 2-2.5 months (longer than assumed)
- Previous assumption (Antonio, Interview 008): 4-6 weeks
- Maciej's experience: "usually 2, maybe 2.5 months" as standard
- Can extend further when knowledge gaps trigger additional Q&A rounds
- **Product implication:** Our 4-6 week engagement fits WITHIN the DD window. We could be deployed as part of the DD process itself.

### Founder retention economics
- 80-90% of Maciej's cases are EXIT transactions (founder wants out)
- Retention period: typically 12-24 months
- Buyers are "quite generous" — €20-40K/month advisory agreements not uncommon
- "The best motivation is keeping shares in hands" — but most founders want full exit
- **Product implication:** Our knowledge extraction could directly compress the retention period. €15-25K for 4-6 weeks vs €720K+ for 24-month advisory agreement at €30K/month.

### Reps & Warranties insurance becoming standard
- Cost: 1-3% of enterprise value
- Limits seller liability to as low as €1
- PE buyers sometimes pay the full cost for clean execution
- **Product implication:** R&W insurance covers "what if something goes wrong." Our product reduces the probability of that by making knowledge transparent. Complementary, not competitive.

### Polish market specifics
- Economy transformed in early 90s → first/second generation businesses now approaching succession
- Founders selling YOUNGER: early 50s now vs late 60s-70s previously. Mindset shift toward work-life balance.
- Poland is primarily M&A inbound (foreign buyers acquiring Polish)
- Polish buyers tend to be opportunistic: "I will wait, they go bankrupt, I buy for €1"
- Strategic acquirers pay best prices (synergies). Financial sponsors want to buy cheaply (IRR driven).
- ~80% of deals go through competitive auction process

### "The business is like another child"
- First/second generation Polish founders have spent 30-40 years building companies
- "It's not about the money. It's about who will buy the company, will they fire people."
- Confirms Pattern 14 (family emotion as deal factor) in Polish context
- **Implication:** Frame our product to sellers as "preserve what you built" — not deal optimization, but legacy preservation.
