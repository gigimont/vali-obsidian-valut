## What We Learned

### The tool-making expert bottleneck (Pattern 2 confirmed again)
- Senior man in the tool-making shop holds 20-30 years of profile knowledge in his head
- Given any drawing: instantly knows if they've done something similar, whether it's technically challenging, what machine/parameters to use
- Retiring in 2-3 years — "it's a huge risk, it's a big issue"
- Process is entirely analog: printed drawings, scribbles, stamps. No digital record of his decisions.
- **Cross-reference:** Identical pattern to Bergerhoff (016) — both are technical experts whose knowledge is the bottleneck. The pattern holds across precast concrete and aluminum extrusion. This is industry-agnostic.

### Hubi's planned approach (useful competitive intelligence)
- Partnering with an old colleague who runs an AI consulting company for manufacturing (~100km away)
- Phase 1: Get all drawings into a database, use AI to find similar past profiles (the "easy part")
- Phase 2: Annotate the decisions — WHY this profile was produced on this machine with these parameters (the "very challenging" part)
- Phase 3: Try to build AI that can replicate the expert's judgment
- Timeline: next few months
- **Implication:** Hubi is building this himself with a partner. He's not waiting for a vendor. If we want AWW as a customer, we'd need to either be faster/better than his DIY approach or partner with his existing plan.

### Pre-transformation documentation as GTM angle (NEW)
- Hubi's consulting background gives him a unique perspective: big ERP transformation projects are expensive and often fail
- Having clear process documentation BEFORE the consultants arrive would be "extremely helpful" — both for the consultants and for the AI tools the consultants will use
- "Not only for the consulting coming in, but also for the AI tools the consulting will be using to further push the transformation"
- **Product implication:** This is a distinct GTM angle we haven't fully explored: sell the knowledge extraction as PREPARATION for digital transformation, not as a standalone succession product. Frame it as: "do your homework before you hire McKinsey."
- **Cross-reference:** Bergerhoff (016) independently said the same: "Eigentlich ist ja die Frage, ob ich das nicht schon als Grundlage brauche, um auch weitere KI-Prozesse zu implementieren" (isn't this the foundation I need for further AI implementation?). Two independent sources arriving at the same "foundation for AI" framing.

### "Celonis for analog companies" resonated
- Wolf's framing: "Celonis plugs in and mines digital processes. We do archaeology — we dig for the knowledge that was never digital."
- Hubi understood and engaged with this immediately
- **This could be a pitch-deck-ready comparison.** Simple, concrete, differentiated.

### Consulting disruption creates market timing
- Hubi (ex-Bearing Point): "It's the first time in consulting that they really have a disruption from AI"
- Analyst-level work is being replaced by AI — companies don't want to pay for it anymore
- This creates an opening: if traditional consulting is being disrupted, a leaner, AI-powered knowledge extraction service can enter at a lower price point
- **Cross-reference:** Armin (017) said "you're just making all the consultants unemployed — that's where they make all the money." Same observation from a different angle.

### The AI consulting buddy (potential CTO / partner lead)
- Hubi's friend runs a custom AI tools company for manufacturing (~100km from AWW)
- They build databases, AI use cases, production-grade tools
- Hubi offered to connect us — potentially for a joint meeting
- **This is a warm intro to someone who is technically doing what we need a CTO to do.** Worth pursuing as either a CTO candidate, a technical advisor, or a partnership.

## Patterns Confirmed

- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 2 — Knowledge trapped:** Tool-making expert with 20-30 years in his head. Industry-agnostic confirmation (aluminum extrusion joins precast concrete, steel manufacturing, glass manufacturing, dairy chemicals, textile).
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 22 — ERP ceiling:** AWW has an ERP but the tool-making expert's knowledge sits entirely outside it. Confirms Bergerhoff's (016) sharpening: digital maturity doesn't solve the tacit knowledge problem.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 25 — Under-advised on AI:** Hubi is actively trying to build AI solutions but doing it DIY — no vendor has offered what he needs.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 26 — Labor scarcity makes knowledge protection more valuable:** The expert is retiring, and finding a replacement with 20-30 years of profile knowledge is impossible.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 27 — Onboarding speed as metric:** Implicit throughout — new employees can't access the expert's knowledge without bothering him directly.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 34 — Standardgrundlage first:** Hubi's phased approach (database of drawings first → AI matching → then judgment annotation) mirrors Bergerhoff's sequencing exactly. Second independent confirmation of Pattern 34.

### No new patterns — but a GTM angle worth noting

**"Pre-transformation documentation"** is a distinct sales framing: not succession, not M&A, but preparation for digital transformation. Two sources (Bergerhoff + Hubi) independently arrived at this framing. This could be a third application on the ICP slide alongside "Preserve & Scale" and "Acquire & Exit."
