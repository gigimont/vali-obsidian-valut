## The Story

### The grandfather's innovation (1949)
- Founded in Bavaria by a Romanian-German refugee after WWII
- Core idea: "replace material by construction" — save resources (sustainability concept in 1949)
- Invented the lattice girder: lightweight steel beam for concrete ceilings
- Borrowed welding technology from aircraft manufacturing (cross-industry innovation)
- Killer application: system so light two people could carry it, no cranes needed — perfect for post-war Germany
- "It took the market like a wildfire" — now 70-80% market share for housing ceilings in Germany

### Joerg's entry (1992)
- Came from Arthur Andersen (Big Five consulting)
- Company was in crisis: negative equity, "clinically dead," father's personal life in turmoil
- Father backed the company with all personal assets to avoid insolvency
- Father was a patriarch and control freak — zero cooperation
- "There was ZERO know-how transferred from my father down to me."
- "I once told him: father, I know that with your experience you can kill all my ideas, but I know that I'm right."
- Created physical distance (Eastern Germany, then Poland) to have freedom to operate

### Building Poland (1992–2011)
- Chose Poland: biggest Central European market (40M people)
- First partner was fraudulent — nearly bled to death
- Had to move to Poland physically: "you have to be present to understand what's going on"
- Planned 2 years. Stayed 19.
- Found his "brother in arms": Grzegorz, a 24-year-old local who became co-CEO
- Built business client by client with private SME customers — state-owned companies were impossible (corrupt)
- Had to diversify products unlike Germany where monoculture worked
- Became important player in Polish reinforcement steel

### Russia and Ukraine (failed expansions)
- Russia: technology worked (1M m² of ceiling in Moscow), failed due to corruption
- Ukraine: same technology, even more corrupt. Pulled out after being openly solicited for bribes at the Ministry of Construction.
- Both: "everything was there — market, need, technology — but it failed because all the other components did not sum up"

### Current situation
- Polish company may not survive current construction downturn
- Nearly liquidated it in a panic, reversed course at the last minute
- Joerg has been through severe burnout and depression — was "gone for years"
- "I'm done with operations. It was never my strong thing."
- Wants to sell or find a successor

## What We Learned

### On zero knowledge transfer (the patriarch pattern)
- Joerg's father was a patriarch who controlled everything and shared nothing
- Same pattern Stefan described independently — confirmed by two brothers
- Joerg had to physically leave to have room to make decisions
- "If we could have worked together, man, the world would have been our playground"

### On knowledge hoarding by employees
- Key employee ("the face of Filigran") held all market knowledge organically in her head
- Deliberately refused to share: "knowledge is power"
- Knowledge was completely unstructured — accumulated through decades
- Confirms self-preservation pattern from [[Interview 011 — Dairy Chemicals Commercial (Italy)]] from the employer's perspective

### On Wolf's experience (the project's origin)
- When Wolf went to Poland, Joerg was in burnout/depression and unable to help
- Grzegorz (partner) was smart but "totally unstructured" and lacked patience to teach
- Wolf was "totally lost" — had to learn by doing with no support
- **The product they're building is the product Wolf needed and didn't have**

### On the founder-to-manager transition
- "That's when the founder needs to leave and the manager needs to come in"
- The entrepreneurial/chaotic phase must transition to structured management at scale
- Joerg knows he's not a manager: "I hate hierarchies. I hate to control people."

### On YPO as a distribution channel
- YPO: ~70,000 members globally, focused on Mittelstand
- Already spoke to Armin Stuttmeyer (Hanover, family business in Germany + US)
- Knows a Berlin-based investor who is a "multiplier"
- Offered to connect through Berlin YPO chapter
- **Caveat:** YPO has a non-solicitation rule. Frame as university research.

### On the one-pager
- Found earlier version not concise enough
- "They have a very short attention span. It needs to grab their attention."
- Offered to review a revised version and distribute to network

### On storytelling (Pattern 28 confirmation)
- "Storytelling is better than a spreadsheet" — said independently of Kevin's campfire insight
- Two independent sources now confirming the same methodology
