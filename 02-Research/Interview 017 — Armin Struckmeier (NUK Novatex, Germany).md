## What We Learned

### The Olive Tree framework (how succession actually works)
- "Like an olive tree that grew organically over 30-40 years. There is no way you can replace that."
- Solution: cut it in pieces. Break the role into manageable units, find someone for each piece.
- Concrete plan for 2028-29: split the German company into a production company + a product-independent sales/wholesale company
- Finding a factory manager: easy. Finding a sales director: easy. Finding someone who can do BOTH (like Armin): impossible.
- Same approach for US operations: split the departing manager's role into accounting, key client relationships, etc.
- **Product implication:** Our knowledge extraction could MAP the olive tree — identify which branches exist, which live in one person's head, and help plan the cut. This is a concrete deliverable.
- **Cross-reference:** Complements [[Interview 016 — Stefan Bergerhoff (BWB-Gruppe, Germany)]] Pattern 35 (three trust thresholds). Bergerhoff maps the buyer complexity WITHIN a company; Armin maps how to SPLIT the irreplaceable role across multiple people.

### Rich or King (Noam Wasserman, Harvard)
- Classic framework: founders who want to stay king (keep control) can't become rich (scale the company)
- Armin hit this ceiling at 120 employees. Restructured into 40-50 person units.
- Self-reflection on limitations is the key differentiator between companies that survive transitions and those that don't.
- "We're not all Maradonas. I'm just a Kreisliga player who was good enough."

### Life Book — the extraction methodology already exists (CRITICAL LEAD)
- Life Book is a YPO company that professionally interviews people and writes their life stories
- Armin did one for his father's birthday — "10-15-20,000 dollar page books"
- They are now using AI to make the process faster
- Andrea (Armin's sister-in-law / brother's wife) manages it
- "Those are the people you need to talk to — what they are using."
- **CRITICAL:** Life Book has already solved "how do you harvest stories efficiently" — the exact methodology gap we're trying to fill. They are either a competitor, a partner, or a methodology source. Contact Andrea immediately.
- **Cross-reference:** Bergerhoff (016) said standard-answers come before stories (Pattern 34). Life Book is a pure story-harvesting operation. The product may need BOTH layers depending on industry: Bergerhoff's normative-first approach for technical domains + Life Book's story-first approach for relational/leadership domains.

### The "mechanical" vs "human" distinction
- Armin cleanly splits business knowledge:
  - "Mechanical" (legal, accounting, ERP, data): "That's the easy stuff. Six-week course."
  - "Human" (psychology, stress management, reading people, micro-expressions, meeting dynamics): "Something you need to experience."
- If you formalise the mechanical layer, you FREE UP bandwidth for the human layer
- **Cross-reference:** Bergerhoff (016) said the same thing differently — his "Standardgrundlage" IS the mechanical layer; his "Sonderfälle" ARE the human judgment layer. Same two-layer model, different vocabulary.

### Ariana's trajectory (the boomerang successor)
- Left after 1.5 years ("Oh my God, this is not for me")
- Went to UK, master's in digital marketing, Amazon startup in London, head of sales at 30 (£85M, 15-person team)
- Came back voluntarily at 30 with startup experience — now controls all e-commerce
- Will NOT be CEO — "doesn't motivate her"
- The olive tree split could solve this: give her a piece that fits her.

### Eva Hart — cautionary tale
- First-generation German company that went bankrupt ~8 months ago
- Market hollowed out: discount grew, luxury grew, middle disappeared
- "They couldn't adjust quick enough to the change in the market"
- Not a succession failure — a market failure. Even well-run family businesses can die from external disruption.
