Plus: explicit endorsement of **shadowing > interviews** as the truer extraction method (independent confirmation of methodology); a clean illustration of the **"outside-experience-first" family-business succession-governance rule**; a concrete **9–12-month transition equilibrium** datapoint; and an unprompted suggestion to engage Merantics / Delta Campus / EWOR.

## What Eckert Said About the Problem

### Same diagnosis, energy-industry language

> *"It is not meant — they don't want to withhold this information, but it just does not come to your mind."*
> *(On why interview-only extraction misses ~40–50% of cases.)*

> *"You cannot do just reading something or watching tutorials or whatsoever — they do not exist and it wouldn't work. So for us, it's super important that new employees work closely together with experts. And we figured out that works best if you sit in the same room."*

> *"Most of the time these doors are open. So it is a little bit noisy, it is a little bit loud, but you also hear what the people next to you are doing. And that is one of the most important things how you learn in our industry. We had quite some trouble during COVID."*

Tacit knowledge as physically co-located air-transfer — not a metaphor, a stated operating policy. COVID home-office was actively resisted; critical-infrastructure status granted exceptions to keep people sitting next to each other.

### Independent endorsement of shadowing over interviews

> *"If you sit down with somebody and say: hey, how was the process? How do you do it? — and they can explain. But this is basically 50 to 60% of the cases match this scheme. There's so many special cases and you would need to sit down with them for hours and hours until they really realize: oh yeah, then we have like this customer and for this customer we need to do it different."*

> *"I think if you are sha[dowing] what they're doing, you can really see how many cases there are and how they are handled."*

When Wolf described Solco's three pillars (interviews + computer-use tracking / shadowing + document ingestion), Eckert independently confirmed that shadowing is the most important pillar. Third independent confirmation of the shadowing primacy claim (after David Richter Interview 025, and Marco Nortmeier Interview 022 / pilot session).

### ERP ceiling in energy — Pattern 22 cross-industry

> *"We were using SAP and SAP was a very individualized version of SAP. So basically a lot of our processes had to be adapted to the single source of truth, which is the SAP system. This worked very well for the time before we were big into trading and hedging. Afterwards, it was very complicated to design processes that are built on the foundation that came out of the SAP system."*

> *"It would have been perfect to have like a clear sheet of paper and starting to design it new, but that's not how it works. So it is like part and part and part and part. And the more complicated it gets, the more risky it gets, and the more failures that happen."*

Identical pattern to Filigran, BWB, AWW, Müller-Menrad: a fully grown ERP that locks the old reality and forces the new business model to bolt on awkwardly. Adds the energy / liquid-fuels industry to the cross-industry list.

### 9–12-month transition equilibrium

> *"3/4 to one year, because it kind of really felt like 1 unit again."*

Concrete number for "how long does generational + business-model shift take to re-equilibrate" in a mid-sized family-business context. Useful for calibrating Solco timeline messaging.

### Off-the-shelf tooling gap

> *"We do not have a lot of tools that you can buy off the shelf. So we have a lot of tools handling our logistics, our tank storage and so on, either built by ourselves or our legacy tools. A lot of that is happening actually in Excel still."*

Direct echo of [[Interview 016 — Stefan Bergerhoff (BWB-Gruppe, Germany)]] (digitalization paradox) and [[Interview 014 — Ulrich Bauermeister (Filigran, Germany)]] (homemade Python+vector-DB). Industry-agnostic: Mittelstand specialists build in Excel because nothing else fits.

## What Eckert Said About the Product (Unprompted Use Cases)

### NEW buyer audience: the Beirat / shareholder board

> *"For example, we have a Beirat, which is a board basically. And the board would love to have these systems visualized or documented. It would help them to also understand in the communication with the managing directors where focus should be, where problems originate, how they get through the system."*

This is **a third buyer audience**, distinct from the CEO/founder buyer and the head-of-department/operational buyer. Boards want a transparent, queryable view into operations so they can (a) follow managing-director reasoning, (b) identify bottlenecks, (c) align stakeholder communication. Aligns with Pattern 44 *passive record* mode. The legitimacy of a Beirat asking for this is high — it is their fiduciary role.

### Managing-director level as the sweet spot — confirmed by an heir

> *"It's that managing director level. That's the really interesting one, because it's far enough from operations to be doable and it's not at the very top to be hypersensitive."*

Pattern 42 articulated unprompted by Eckert, in the same words Solco would use. Cross-industry confirmation in energy. Plus the implicit corollary: *top-of-house knowledge is too sensitive to map, front-line is too granular, middle (head-of-dept / MD) is the value-bearing layer.*

### Adjacent use case: value-driver discovery in a black-box value chain

> *"In our case, what we also where we have a lack of clarity is when we look at the whole value chain that we are handling. So where are really the — where the profit is really originating. It's a little bit like a black box. You see what goes in there, you see what goes out there, and you have, you see that it is profitable and it's good and you're continuing to doing that — but identifying where the real value drivers are in this black box. This is something that is not so easy. And we have a person that is basically dedicated to find that out for different processes."*

> *"In the model, it would be interesting. I'm not 100% sure if it would be easy, but it would be interesting to also search for value drivers in the process chain."*

BMV Energy has a dedicated **process / value-driver discovery role** (background: consulting, possibly data scientist). Eckert sees Solco's second brain as a candidate tool to do this work systematically. This is the [[Product Methodology]] *vulnerability inventory* concept extended: the same context layer that captures tacit judgment can be queried for *where value actually originates* and *where the load-bearing single points of failure live*.

> *(Giuseppe, in response):* *"that's also one of the aim, of course, one of the biggest like value points... a sort of vulnerability inventory, which basically aims to find in the company where are the most critical parts in which actually the value is generated and in terms of who owns the majority of the knowledge that continues to push forward the company."*

Eckert: *"that is also, of course, interesting for the management, for the managing directors, but also for the controlling guys."*

**Three audiences for the value-driver layer:** management, board, controlling.
