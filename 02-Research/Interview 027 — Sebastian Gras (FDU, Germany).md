# Interview 027 — Sebastian Gras (FDU / B.B.T., Germany)

#research #interview #customer-side #mittelstand #precast #multi-site #pattern-46 #pattern-47

> **Date:** May 26, 2026
> **Interviewee:** Sebastian Gras — Geschäftsführer at B.B.T. Plan und Statik (sister company of F.D.U. GmbH) since 2019; 10 years at FDU group total
> **Company:** F.D.U. (family-owned by Familie Brinkhegel), Georgsmarienhütte (Lower Saxony). ~30 Betonwerke / Fertigteilwerke across Germany. Product: full and semi-precast concrete elements (Decken, Wände, Treppen, Balkone, Trempel). ~120 CAD drafters / Bautechniker / Bauingenieure under B.B.T.
> **Format:** Teams call, 38 min, German, transcript available
> **Context:** First customer-side voice from a Filigran customer. Introduction chain: Bauermeister (Filigran Bautechnik) → Bergerhoff (BWB Planungsleiter, Interview 016) → Gras (FDU). Pre-read was the one-pager containing the Bauermeister €20K story — Gras had read it before the call.

## ⚠️ Significance of This Interview

First **customer-side / downstream-buyer voice** confirming the Solcus problem and the Solcus product surface from a 30-plant precast Mittelstand group. Three substantive contributions:

1. **Independent confirmation of Pattern 44 (active retrieval).** Gras articulated the *memory-that-acts* product mode **unprompted** — Q&A interface that returns prior-project answers with named authors (Planungsleiter, Statiker, Architekt). Pattern 44 now has three independent voices: Eric Quintane (academic), Marco Nortmeier (operational), Sebastian Gras (customer leadership). The pattern is hardening fast.
2. **Self-quantified recurring-process rate (~85-90%).** Gras volunteered the number without prompting. This is a Solcus-grade ROI anchor for the precast / construction vertical: 85-90% of work flows through patterns that can be matched, leaving 10-15% genuine exception load for human judgment. NEW Pattern 47.
3. **Cross-site knowledge silos as a new ICP vector.** FDU runs ~30 plants across Germany; identical problems recur in Öhringen, Kopp (near Kiel), and Velten (near Berlin) with **no mandatory cross-site exchange**. Multi-site Mittelstand groups have an additional product surface beyond single-site succession risk: a cross-plant knowledge layer. NEW Pattern 46.

Also: Gras validates Bauermeister's €20K forgotten-experiment story unprompted ("es ist ja so erschreckend, wie wie einfach, nur wenn keiner weiß, dass das schon mal stattgefunden hat") — Pattern 31 lands across the customer-supplier boundary, which is a strong out-of-network confirmation signal.

## What Gras Said About the Problem

### Same diagnosis, customer-side language
Gras spontaneously reproduced the trapped-knowledge framing without Wolf priming it:

> *"viel Wissen... am Ende des Tages natürlich vorhanden ist, aber eben... entweder in den Köpfen oder eben auf dem Rechner der einzelnen Person."*
> "A lot of knowledge is there, but it lives either in heads or on individual people's computers."

He named the demographic pressure explicitly (workforce in their late fifties, *Fachkräftemangel* making niche replacement harder), then described the succession-cascade dynamic at FDU's acquired plants:

> *"Da geht der erste in Rente. Dann kann es vielleicht noch der zweite abfangen, aber dann geht der zweite in Rente. Da muss es wieder ein jüngerer sich erst mal einarbeiten, und wir stellen dann fest: ja, aber das hat doch immer der und der gemacht."*
> "The first one retires. Maybe the second can still catch it, but then the second retires too. A junior has to learn from scratch, and we realize: yes, but so-and-so always did that."

This is **Pattern 22 (ERP ceiling) read at the M&A integration layer**: every acquired Betonwerk arrives with its own CAD, its own MS / machine-control system, and its own undocumented routines. FDU has been absorbing this knowledge entropy across ~30 sites for years.

### Validation of Pattern 31 (€20K forgotten experiment)
Gras read the one-pager before the call and reacted to the Bauermeister story with visible discomfort:

> *"Es ist ja so erschreckend, wie wie einfach, nur wenn keiner weiß, dass das schon mal stattgefunden hat. Wie soll der Hinweis kommen?"*
> "It's frightening how simple it is — if nobody knows it happened before, where would the prompt even come from?"

A customer-side leader cross-validating a supplier-side pattern strengthens the dataset. Pattern 31 generalizes beyond Filigran.

## What Gras Said About the Product (Unprompted)

### Articulated the active-retrieval product mode himself

This is the single strongest moment in the interview. Gras described the use case Solcus is building **before Wolf described it**:

> *"Eine Art Q&A... ich stelle die Frage und dann sagt er mir aus meiner Datenbank heraus: alles klar, in der Vergangenheit, das ist ein Beispielprojekt, das wurde von X.Y.Z. beantwortet oder bearbeitet, wurde es wie folgt gelöst — mit dem und dem Hinweis von einem Planungsleiter, von einem Statiker, vom Architekten. Das ist ja genau der Punkt, wo wir perspektivisch hinmüssen, damit man im Kern unabhängig von Ansprechpartnern wird."*
> "A kind of Q&A — I ask the question and it answers from the database: this was a similar project, X.Y.Z. solved it like this, with these notes from the Planungsleiter, the structural engineer, the architect. That's exactly where we need to go perspectively — to be at the core independent of contact persons."

Independent of contact persons. That is the Pattern 44 (memory that acts) value proposition in customer language.

### Temporal decoupling as a concrete daily use case

> *"Wenn jetzt jemand sagt, ich fange morgens um 07:00 Uhr schon an und der Ansprechpartner fängt erst um 08:30 Uhr an, dann ist er anderthalb Stunden im Vakuum. Somit hätte er da schon mal den Ansatz und könnte weiterarbeiten."*
> "Someone starts at 07:00 and their go-to contact doesn't start until 08:30 — they're in a vacuum for an hour and a half. With this they'd have an entry point and could keep working."

This is Pattern 44 made concrete: not abstract decision-support, but the literal early-morning operator with no one to ask.

### The 85-90% recurring-process self-quantification

> *"Wir machen, ist ja sehr individuell. Nichtsdestotrotz hat man ja gewisse Muster, die immer und immer und immer wieder auftreten... ich hoffe vielleicht etwas niedrigere Zahlen nennen, das kann sein, aber ich sag mal wahrscheinlich 85 bis 90% haben wir wiederkehrende Prozesse."*
> "We're individual — but you have patterns that keep recurring. Maybe I'm being generous, but I'd say 85 to 90% of our processes are recurring."

A head-of-department who quantifies the repetition rate himself is a head-of-department who can compute his own ROI on Solcus. NEW Pattern 47.

### Failure archive as adjacent value

> *"Auch ehrlicherweise auch, wenn es nicht funktioniert hat oder beziehungsweise zu Problemen führte. Wie hat man es gemacht und wie sollte man es nicht wieder machen?"*
> "Also honestly when something didn't work or led to problems. How was it done, and how should we not do it again?"

Solcus deliverable expansion: not just "what to copy" but "what to never repeat." Negative-knowledge archive.

### Cross-site silo as a multi-plant pain point (NEW)

> *"Vielleicht mal in Öhringen bei unserem Werk, dann in Kopp bei unserem Werk bei Kiel und dann wiederum in Velten bei Berlin. Und da ist ja nicht dieser zwingende Austausch."*
> "Maybe in Öhringen at our plant, then at Kopp near Kiel, then in Velten near Berlin. And there's no mandatory exchange between them."

30 plants, recurring problems, no horizontal pipe between them. NEW Pattern 46. Different value-creation logic than the single-site succession story: cross-plant knowledge aggregation as a multi-site Mittelstand specific surface.

## How Wolf Pitched (and What Stuck)

### Methodology-as-product framing landed with no pushback

> Wolf: *"Wir verkaufen nicht irgendwie ein Tool oder irgendeine Plattform... es ist wirklich eher diese Dienstleistung, um intern... das spezialisierte zweite Gehirn eines Unternehmens zu entwickeln."*
> "We're not selling a tool or platform — it's actually a service to develop the internal specialised second brain of the company."

This is the v6 consulting-practice identity (see [[Problem Statement]]) delivered in German for the first time. Gras registered it without friction. Language is starting to crystallize for the DACH customer audience.

### Documentation precedes AI in the staging

Wolf explicitly led with the recording/structuring layer, deferred AI talk to "later":

> *"Erst geht es um die Dokumentation, um die Aufzeichnung von dem, was es schon gibt... auf eine strukturierte Art in diese Kontextschicht reinzutun, sodass es zugreifbar ist. Erst später wird über K.I. Anwendungen geredet."*
> "First it's about documentation, recording what already exists, structuring it into the context layer so it's accessible. Only later do we talk about AI applications."

Wolf names the reason: the *psychology of the workforce*. Lead with AI and you trigger the replacement-fear cluster (Patterns 21 / 37 / 39 / 45-adjacent). Lead with documentation and people stay engaged. Confirms the Filigran sequencing decision.

### Tool stack discussion (Gras asked)

Gras asked which AI Wolf uses. Wolf named:
- **Claude (Anthropic)** — main driver, business-direct tone, Claude Code in the terminal for vault automation
- **Gemini (Google)** — softer, used for text smoothing / writing
- **Mistral** — European LLM, evaluated specifically for data-protection compliance and handwriting transcription

This is the first interview where the data-sovereignty / European-LLM angle was explicitly framed as a deliberate part of the stack. Useful for any future DPO conversation.

## Strategic Implications

1. **Customer-side validation closes the loop.** Solcus has now heard the same problem stated by: the technical insider (Bauermeister), the operational leader (Marco Nortmeier), the customer-side Planungsleiter (Bergerhoff), and the customer-side Geschäftsführer (Gras). Four layers of the same diagnosis. Pattern 44 (active retrieval) has been articulated by three of them without Wolf prompting. The thesis can be defended cold.
2. **Multi-site Mittelstand is a new ICP wedge candidate.** FDU's 30-plant structure makes cross-site aggregation more valuable than single-site succession. Larger deal sizes, harder relationship-building, but a clearer ROI story (replicate one site's pattern library across 29 others).
3. **Lead-with-documentation, defer-AI is the right pitch sequence.** Wolf used it deliberately and Gras tracked smoothly. Pattern 28 (storytelling welcome, documentation resisted) was avoided by reframing documentation as recording + structuring, not as a compliance exercise.
4. **Mistral / European-LLM angle is starting to matter.** First interview where Wolf used data-sovereignty as an active sales point. Worth formalising as a competitive differentiator vs US-only competitors.
5. **The 85-90% number wants to be a pitch slide.** Single source so far, but Gras volunteered it. If it survives one or two more interviews, it goes on the deck as "85% of mid-market manufacturing work is pattern-recurrent — Solcus turns those patterns into a queryable layer."
