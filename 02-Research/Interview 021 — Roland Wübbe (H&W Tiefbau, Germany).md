## ⚠️ Significance of This Interview

**Roland is the first interviewee in the dataset who has personally completed TWO full succession events from opposite sides.** In 1988, at age 31, he received the company from his father with what amounts to the archetypal unstructured handover ("ich habe keine Lust mehr, lass uns zum Notar gehen, du wirst Geschäftsführer"). In 2024, he completed the opposite: a deliberately structured 1.5-year transition to his designated successor Karsten Böing. This makes him a meta-witness — the only voice in our dataset who has lived both sides of the generational handover and consciously reformed the process.

**Three distinct contributions emerged from this interview, each significant in its own right:**

1. **Third independent prospect telling us our succession framing is too narrow.** Roland, like Bergerhoff ([[Interview 016 — Stefan Bergerhoff (BWB-Gruppe, Germany)]]) and Hubi ([[Interview 018 — Hubertus von Huelsen (AWW, Germany)]]), independently arrived at the conclusion that the value proposition extends far beyond succession into onboarding, organizational documentation, and general operations. Three is a pattern, not a coincidence. This is now a strategic signal requiring a positioning decision.

2. **The most thoughtful pushback we have received yet on product consequences.** Roland raised an argument no other interviewee has made: materializing intuition into AI risks atrophy of the underlying human skill. This is not a feasibility objection (Newton, [[Interview 010 — Newton Campos (Search Fund)]]) or a self-preservation objection ([[Interview 011 — Dairy Chemicals Commercial (Italy)]]). It is a consequence objection — what happens to organizations once they depend on the captured artifact instead of training the human capability. This needs a structured response, and now forms an objection cluster with Newton (Pattern 21) and Francis de Vericourt (Pattern 39, [[Interview 020 — Francis de Vericourt (ESMT Professor)]]).

3. **A structural correction to our understanding of H&W Teampower.** Wolf had previously framed Teampower (in the Jana and Roland outreach emails) as a knowledge-distribution mechanism. Roland gently corrected this: Teampower is a tax-efficient capital participation model that isolates equipment ownership from operational risk. Not our pattern. See "Teampower: What It Actually Is" below.

## The Two Successions

### Succession 1 — Father to Roland (1988)

Roland was 31, had been in the business one year, having previously worked two years elsewhere after his civil engineering degree (RWTH Aachen, 1977 Abitur).

> "Mein Vater hat gesagt, du, ich hab keine Lust mehr, lass uns zum Notar gehen, du wirst jetzt Geschäftsführer."

The succession event was:
- Single notary visit, no transition period
- No formal communication to customers, suppliers, or partners
- Roland's father was 62; motivation was personal ("ich habe keine Lust mehr")

The immediate consequence: Gelsenwasser AG — their most important customer — called weeks later, "etwas pikiert," that the change had not been communicated. The personal-relationship layer broke the moment the personal handler changed, and the new handler did not know to repair it.

This is the canonical version of the unstructured Mittelstand handover. Roland's father had built the business on personal relationships but had not built any meta-process for transferring those relationships — likely because he did not see them as a process at all.

### Succession 2 — Roland to Karsten Böing (2024)

Roland's own succession, 36 years later, was deliberately the opposite:

- **Early successor identification.** Karsten Böing had already been with the company 15–16 years. His father had been a Prokurist. He joined directly after his dual studies (done at co-shareholder Hülscher's company).
- **1.5-year designated successor period.** Karsten was named Geschäftsführer 1.5 years before Roland's exit, after years as Prokurist and technischer Leiter.
- **Pre-existing structural integration.** Karsten's career was built entirely inside the H&W ecosystem from his early 20s, so the relational network transferred organically.
- **Ownership and operational leadership explicitly separated.** Wübbe family share is held by a GmbH (Wübbe Vermögensverwaltung), with Roland's son Leonhard as Geschäftsführer of the holding. This preserves family ownership without requiring family members to lead operationally.

**Implication:** The before-and-after comparison within a single company is the cleanest example in our dataset of what good structural succession looks like — and the persistent gap that even good structural succession does not close. The structural reforms transferred ownership and operational control. They could not transfer the intuition layer.

## What We Learned

### Teampower: what it actually is (CORRECTION TO PRIOR FRAMING)

Roland gently corrected an assumption Wolf had carried from earlier outreach. Teampower is not a knowledge distribution mechanism. It is a tax-efficient employee capital participation model.

The actual mechanism:
- All equipment and machinery sits in a separate Kommanditgesellschaft (H&W Teampower)
- Teampower rents equipment to the operating company at a fixed monthly rate covering repair costs
- Employees can become Mitunternehmer in Teampower with interest-free company loans
- Reduced repair costs flow as profits to employee co-owners; employees earn 10–50% annual returns with near-zero risk (operating-side risk — weather, miscalculation, non-payment — stays in the operating entity)
- Tax-efficient vs bonuses: a 1€ bonus costs ~1.40€ employer-side and arrives as ~0.60€ at the employee; Teampower distributions arrive whole, taxed only at individual rate
- The co-shareholder (Hülscher) developed this into a commercial offering, selling the legal construct to other firms through industry associations — inspired originally by a Hamburg port logistics operation

**What this means for our project:** Teampower does not validate or relate to our knowledge-transfer thesis. We should not lean on it in pitch materials. Correct any prior outreach materials that implied otherwise.

### The atrophy critique (Pattern 37 — objection cluster member)

Roland raised an argument no prior interviewee had made:

> "Die Intuition ist ja eine Fähigkeit, die immer trainiert werden muss. Wenn ich das dann abgebe an eine KI... dann laufe ich Gefahr, dass diese Fähigkeit verkümmert."

The argument: if expertise becomes accessible via AI, the humans who would have developed that expertise stop training the underlying skill. The organization gains a captured artifact but loses its capacity to produce the next generation of intuition. The artifact ages; the producer atrophies.

This forms an objection cluster with two related-but-distinct concerns from other interviews:
- **Pattern 21 — Newton (Interview 010):** "Retroactive capture is not feasible."
- **Pattern 37 — Roland (this interview):** "Even what you do capture risks atrophying the underlying human capability."
- **Pattern 39 — Francis (Interview 020):** "The capture rate is partial; overclaiming it damages credibility."

All three deserve a unified positioning response. Candidate framing: *"We capture transferable frameworks, not the full intuition. Our deliverable reduces dependency on bottleneck experts and accelerates onboarding — but it is designed to scaffold human judgment, not substitute for it. The people doing the work continue to develop their judgment on real situations; our artifact is a reference, not an answer-generator."*

**Action:** Develop this response before the next high-stakes conversation (Hamburg, investor meeting, or CTO candidate).

### Third independent framing-too-narrow signal

Roland: *"Sie hängen das Ganze an dem Thema Unternehmensnachfolge auf. Aber es geht ja eigentlich weit darüber hinaus. Wenn das mal funktioniert, funktioniert's ja eigentlich überall."*

This is now confirmed by three independent prospects across three industries:
- **Bergerhoff (016):** ecosystem foundation framing — succession is just one of many triggers
- **Hubi (018):** pre-transformation documentation framing — succession, M&A, and digital transformation
- **Roland (021):** broadest statement — "if it works, it works everywhere"

**Tension with Francis (Interview 020):** Francis says go narrower for proof of concept. Roland, Bergerhoff, and Hubi say the framing is too narrow. These are not contradictory — they operate on different axes:
- **Bergerhoff, Hubi, Roland:** "The application universe is broader than succession alone."
- **Francis:** "For your initial proof of concept, go narrow. Build one thing that works first."

Synthesis: broad applicability story on the pitch deck application slide + one narrow proof of concept as the lead. Both true simultaneously.

### Structural separation of ownership and operational leadership (Pattern 38)

The Wübbe Vermögensverwaltung GmbH structure preserves family ownership via a holding entity while operational leadership transfers cleanly to a non-family executive (Karsten Böing). Leonhard (son, Facharbeiterausbildung, unfinished studies) has meaningful roles — Projektleiter at H&W, Geschäftsführer of both the holding company and Teampower — without being forced into an operational leadership role he is not qualified for.

This is a structural answer to the "family successor not suitable for CEO" pattern visible across multiple interviews (Vetreria 007, Filigran 012, NUK 017). The Wübbe model shows it is solvable without conflict or value destruction.

### Peter May organization and Hamburg Family Summit

Roland offered two high-value network references:

### New

**Pattern 37 — The atrophy risk: captured intuition can erode the capacity to produce new intuition**
Materializing tacit knowledge into AI-accessible form creates a substitution effect on human skill development. The next generation, with the artifact available, may stop training the underlying judgment that produced it. The organization gains a captured asset but loses its production capability for that asset over time. Distinct from Newton's feasibility objection (Pattern 21) and the partial-capture honesty principle (Pattern 39, Francis) — this is a long-term consequence objection. Forms an objection cluster with Patterns 21 and 39; requires a unified positioning response before next high-stakes conversation.
- **Source:** [[Interview 021 — Roland Wübbe (H&W Tiefbau, Germany)]]
- **Strength:** Moderate — single source, but the most thoughtful structural pushback in the dataset from a doubly experienced successor

**Pattern 38 — Structural separation of ownership and operational succession**
Family ownership can be preserved through a holding GmbH while operational leadership transfers cleanly to a non-family executive. Solves the "family successor not suitable for CEO" problem visible across multiple interviews without forcing the fit or creating conflict. Not a product feature but an adjacent succession architecture pattern worth flagging in conversations with founders facing the same dilemma.
- **Source:** [[Interview 021 — Roland Wübbe (H&W Tiefbau, Germany)]], retroactive relevance to [[Interview 007 — Vetreria Rachello (Italy)]] and [[Interview 012 — Stefan Weiler (Filigran, Germany)]]
- **Strength:** Moderate — well-developed single example; benefits from cross-referencing
