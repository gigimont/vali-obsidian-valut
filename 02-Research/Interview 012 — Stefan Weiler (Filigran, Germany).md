### The generational arc
- **1st gen (grandfather):** Founded 1949, invented the product, built from zero as a post-war refugee
- **2nd gen (Stefan's father):** Patriarch. Controlled every decision including buying pencils. Not a salesperson — delegated sales to a "very experienced right hand" woman. Knew some clients but was "more of a loner."
- **3rd gen (Stefan + brother):** Entered 1992 and 1997. Changed leadership style dramatically — delegated heavily, took over client and supplier relationships personally. Built personal relationships with all key clients, suppliers, and even competitors.

## What We Learned

### On knowledge transfer (zero structure, pure osmosis)
- Three generations, zero structured onboarding for any of them
- Learning method: "open your eyes, open your ears, ask questions" — and that's it
- No documentation of how decisions are made, how clients are managed, how steel is purchased
- Economic data is in databases (since 1998). Everything else is in people's heads.
- Stefan explicitly confirmed: "We do not have a plan to preserve knowledge."

### On the leadership transition (patriarch → delegator)
- Father controlled everything — a patriarch who made every decision personally
- Stefan and his brother deliberately changed the style: delegated budgets, gave responsibility, focused themselves on the two things that matter most: buying steel (65-70% of costs) and managing key client/supplier relationships
- This transition was NOT planned — it emerged from necessity ("we don't have time for that, we have more important things to do")
- It took years of trial and error
- **Pattern match:** Same dynamic as [[Interview 005 — Real Estate Agency (Italy)]] — delegation through verticalisation. But Stefan is more explicit that it was a CHOICE to change from his father's style, not a natural continuation.

### On personal relationships as the irreplaceable core
- ~150 customers, all known personally for decades
- 3 salespeople + Stefan himself manage all relationships
- The hard facts (prices, tonnage, delivery history) are in the database
- The personal things ("who likes hunting, who likes beer") are NOT in any system — "very roughly" noted, if at all
- When a salesperson leaves: "you have to rebuild a new relationship. There's no way around that."
- Relationships with competitors are also personal — they can "have a beer" together at trade fairs despite competing

### On succession (wide open, ticking clock)
- 4 shareholders have 8 children total
- Some potentially interested but all still students — "it's quite early"
- Stefan's own 2 children study business administration — "potentially candidates"
- But: "I remember when I was a student, I couldn't care less about the company" — he doesn't expect them to care yet
- Decision window: 2-4 years to decide direction
- Options if no family successor: sell the company OR hire external CEO and train them
- Emotional driver: "we want to keep it running... it's a very personal feeling for the village"

### On market pressure
- Building industry suffering across Germany — "it just became too expensive"
- Was 150 employees, now 110 — market contraction
- Poland sister company may close
- Disruptive technology possible: prefabricated building elements, robotic production, CO2-saving construction methods
- "It's a pretty interesting time now and changing a lot"

### On technology and processes
- Production planning is entirely handmade — not computer-based
- Order flow: email/WhatsApp/phone → manual production scheduling → own truck delivery
- "We don't need really advanced processes... it's very simply structured, like selling bread in the bakery"
- No process management tools beyond the basic database/ERP
- But: has "quite good controlling instruments" — can spot if something goes wrong in any department

## Patterns Confirmed

- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 2 — Knowledge trapped at two levels:** Stefan holds relationship knowledge. Salespeople hold regional client knowledge. Neither is documented beyond basic CRM notes.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 5 — Irreplaceability myth:** "You have to do that personally. There's no way around that." Not a myth here — genuinely personal. Same as Vetreria and real estate.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 12 — Delegation through verticalisation:** Stefan and brother deliberately changed from patriarch to delegated model. Partial solution to the day-to-day but no solution for succession.
- **[[Patterns From Fieldwork]] Pattern 16 — Successor pool shrinks by choice:** 8 potential successors, all still students, none showing active interest yet.
- **Long onboarding by osmosis:** Confirmed again — "just joined and listened and watched." No structured transfer across 3 generations.

### No new patterns — but a notable data point
**This is the first German manufacturing company in the dataset.** All patterns identified in Italian SMEs (knowledge in heads, no succession plan, personal relationships as core value, delegation as partial solution) hold identically in a German industrial context. The thesis is not Italy-specific.
