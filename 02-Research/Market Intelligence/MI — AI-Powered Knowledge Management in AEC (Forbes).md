## Source

- **Forbes / Heather Wishart-Smith** — "Designing and Building With AI-Powered Knowledge Management" (published 2024-06-11). Companion piece to a Society of American Military Engineers (SAME) workshop on AI applications in the AEC industry. The author moderated one of the panels. Stored raw at `00-Raw/Designing and Building With AI-Powered Knowledge Management.md`.

---

## What This Is

A survey article — not a primary research piece — capturing senior-executive viewpoints from Autodesk, WSP, AECOM, DPR Construction, Matrix Design Group, ALICE Technologies, Revaka, and the US Navy Civil Engineer Corps on how AI-powered knowledge management and data analytics are entering the architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) industry. Read it as a **mood-of-the-industry snapshot** for a vertical that ranks near the bottom of digital advancement (2016 McKinsey: 2nd-to-last of 22 industries studied) but is now under acute pressure (labour shortage, demand for infrastructure, sustainability mandates) to digitise.

Useful to Solco as a **parallel-industry reference point**: AEC is structurally similar to Mittelstand manufacturing (analogue, project-based, expert-dependent, low digital maturity) and is one of the industries where the "AI + knowledge management" thesis is being most actively bought today.

## Key Insights Relevant to Solco

### 1. AEC is the second-least-digital industry — same starting point as Mittelstand manufacturing

McKinsey's 2016 ranking placed AEC second-to-last of 22 industries in digital advancement (ahead only of agriculture/hunting). The industry is a $12 trillion global market under simultaneous pressure from:

- Infrastructure demand
- Labour shortages
- Stakeholder demand for modernisation

This pressure stack is **structurally identical** to the German/Italian Mittelstand pressure stack (succession cliff, demographic decline, M&A-driven modernisation, sustainability reporting). The fact that AEC is now actively buying AI knowledge management at scale is direct precedent for Mittelstand adoption.

**Solco angle:** When asked "why would low-digital-maturity SMEs buy AI?", AEC is the answer. They are. Already. At scale. Use the construction comparable in pitches.

### 2. The "30% of designer time lost to information search" benchmark

AECOM's framing (Karl Jensen, EVP National Governments business): designers spend up to **30% of their time** looking for information across proposals, drawings, white papers, and cost estimates. AECOM is building an internal proprietary search system over this corpus, with **citations back to source documents and employee names**.

This is the same benchmark family as [[MI — Why Now Is the Knowledge Management Moment (Atlassian)]] (25% of workweek lost to search) and Pattern 31 (Bauermeister's €20K/forgotten-experiment ROI framing). Three independent sources converging on roughly **25–30% productivity tax from undocumented institutional knowledge.**

**Solco angle:** The "25–30% knowledge-tax" stat is now triangulated across three independent sources (Atlassian, Forbes/AECOM, Bauermeister). Strong enough to use as a headline number in the pitch deck and one-pager. Cite all three so it can't be dismissed as cherry-picking.

### 3. AECOM's "curated internal knowledge + source citations + employee names" is the closest enterprise analogue to Solco's deliverable

AECOM's system:
- Searches internal corpus (proposals, drawings, white papers, cost estimates)
- Stays within the corporate boundary (privacy / IP protection)
- Returns answers **with source material AND employee names**

This is almost exactly the Solco knowledge-base shape:
- Queryable corpus of extracted expert knowledge
- Local / privacy-respecting (Pattern 31, Pattern 35)
- Attribution — provenance trace back to the human source ([[MI — Contextual Blindness and Knowledge Graphs (LinkedIn)]] "provenance trace = trust")

AECOM is an **$8B design firm** building this internally. Validates that the architectural shape is what the market wants. Solco's wedge: deliver the same shape to companies that cannot afford to build it themselves.

**Solco angle:** AECOM is the enterprise upper bound; Solco is the SME-sized version of the same architecture. Concrete reference point for technical investor conversations.

### 4. "The barrier to entry is low; you don't have to be a mega-firm to benefit from AI"

Sal Nodjomian (CEO, Matrix Design Group): explicit statement that small businesses can leverage AI without enterprise budgets. The private sector moves faster than government; industry must lead.

This contradicts the dominant Mittelstand objection ("AI is for big companies / we'll wait and see"). Coming from a Matrix Design CEO at a Navy-sponsored workshop, it has authority weight that a startup pitch lacks.

**Solco angle:** Quotable in any "why now / why us" pitch slide. The argument that small businesses can adopt AI is being made by industry executives in adjacent verticals, not just by AI vendors.

### 6. WSP's three AI initiation principles — directly transferable to a Solco GTM playbook

Pooja Jain (SVP Strategic Innovations, WSP) named three principles for AI initiation:

1. **Embedding responsibility** — governance and AI policy
2. **Prioritising people** — change management, upskilling, training
3. **Data strategy and governance** — data organisation, protection for AI effectiveness, risk avoidance

These map cleanly to the three things every Solco engagement must deliver alongside the knowledge base:

- **Governance** → who can query the knowledge base, who owns it, who can update it (= Pattern 35 trust thresholds)
- **People** → successor / management team / Beirat training on how to use it ([[Interview 028 — Benjamin Eckert (Libergy & BMV Energy, Germany)]] Beirat audience)
- **Data strategy** → what gets extracted, what stays private, what gets exposed to general LLMs (= the privacy moat in [[MI — Knowledge Graphs for Enterprise AI]])

**Solco angle:** Adopt these three principles as the explicit framing for Solco's "engagement deliverable beyond the knowledge base." Three buckets, named by an SVP of a $3B firm, that justify a higher-priced engagement than "just a chatbot."

### 7. "We tend to fear what we don't understand" — education-as-go-to-market is endorsed by an industry CEO

Sal Nodjomian (Matrix Design Group): *"An effective way to reduce fear and increase the implementation of AI is through education and thoughtful discussion."*

This is the explicit endorsement of the "delegation-first / education-first" Solco GTM choice ([[Standup — 2026-05-20]]) — and the strongest external validation we have for the AWW / Filigran seminar model over a hard-sell motion.

**Solco angle:** Pattern 28 (campfire method) and the delegation-first methodology have an authoritative parallel in AEC: the industry has decided that education reduces resistance. Quote Nodjomian in any "why this GTM approach" justification.

## Methodology Notes

- The article is a single-source survey of senior executives at a single workshop — opinion-rich, evidence-light. Read it as **sentiment + named-references**, not as primary research.
- The five companies named (Autodesk, WSP, AECOM, ALICE, Revaka) are all upper-tier players. SME representation is absent. Treat the "barrier to entry is low" claim as aspirational rather than evidenced.
- Author disclosure: Wishart-Smith moderated one of the panels. Mild positive bias toward the workshop framing.
