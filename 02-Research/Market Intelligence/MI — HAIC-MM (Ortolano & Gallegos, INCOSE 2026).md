## What HAIC-MM Is

The Human-AI Collaboration Maturity Model (HAIC-MM) is a validated systems engineering framework for SMEs to assess and improve their AI adoption. It identifies 7 dimensions, 32 capabilities, and 5 maturity levels specific to the constraints of small and medium-sized enterprises.

**The 5 maturity levels:**
1. **AI Exploration** — initial/ad hoc AI awareness
2. **AI-Enabled** — isolated AI tools in use
3. **AI-Embedded** — AI integrated into core workflows
4. **AI-Aligned** — AI strategy aligned with business objectives
5. **Symbiotic** — humans and AI operating as complementary partners

**The 7 dimensions:**
1. AI-Enhanced Leadership & Strategy
2. Adaptive AI Culture & Empowerment
3. Human-Centric AI Integration & Experience
4. Harmonizing AI & Human Processes
5. Human-Centered AI Customer Engagement
6. AI Ethics & Human Oversight
7. Inclusive AI Governance & Learning

**Validation:** Survey N=100, focus groups N=10 (5 sessions, industry practitioners), pilot N=3. Mixed-methods, rigorous. Not just a consulting deck.

## Key Insights Relevant to Solcus

### 1. SME AI barriers are our ICP pain profile, stated academically

The paper lists four SME-specific adoption barriers: (1) tighter budgetary constraints; (2) reduced access to specialized AI expertise; (3) less formalized operations that lack structured data; (4) multi-role employees with no capacity for dedicated AI projects.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** This is the exact profile of our ICP — Filigran, AWW, H&W Tiefbau, Colusso Hardware. These are not accidental patterns. This is a documented structural reality of SMEs. We can cite this in the pitch deck as the academic grounding for why SMEs are systematically under-served by enterprise AI tools.

### 2. "Leadership AI Literacy" = Pattern 25 (under-advised on AI)

The model's first dimension includes "Leadership AI Literacy" as a core capability: "Equip leaders with a deep understanding of AI to champion initiatives and leverage AI effectively." The paper notes this is the most common failure point — leaders can't evaluate AI adoption because they don't understand what AI can do.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Pattern 25 ([[Interview 006 - John Lynch (Lynka)]]) and ([[Interview 018 — Hubertus von Huelsen (AWW, Germany)]]). Hubi specifically said "I don't know what AI can do for me" — the paper names this as the foundational gap. Our extraction methodology starts by surfacing what knowledge exists before the leader ever has to evaluate AI tools. We're solving the precondition to AI adoption, not competing with AI tools.

### 3. "Human Trust in AI Assessment" = Pattern 35 (three trust thresholds)

The model includes a specific capability: "Assess and foster trust in AI through transparency, reliability, and employee involvement." This was added AFTER focus groups flagged it as missing — indicating it's a real-world concern that practitioners independently surfaced.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Pattern 35 ([[Interview 016 — Stefan Bergerhoff (BWB-Gruppe, Germany)]], [[Interview 022 — Marco Nortmeier (Filigran, Germany)]]). Marco's progression from skeptic to verbal pilot yes followed exactly this: transparency about methodology → reliability of output → involvement in the process. The three trust thresholds we observed in field match what HAIC-MM identifies academically.

### 4. "Psychological Safety in AI Collaboration" = Pattern 29 (tech must not feel like tech)

The model explicitly includes "Create a safe environment for employees to interact openly with AI, promoting innovation" as a core capability. Employees fear AI replacing them or exposing their knowledge gaps.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Pattern 29 ([[Interview 013 — David (Textile Manufacturing, Germany)]]). David's "campfire method" intuition was exactly this: make knowledge capture feel like a conversation, not surveillance. Tech fatigue = lack of psychological safety. Our methodology's storytelling-first approach directly addresses this capability.

### 5. The 5 maturity levels as Solcus ICP scoring tool

The maturity ladder (Exploration → Enabled → Embedded → Aligned → Symbiotic) provides a framework for segmenting our ICP. Our current customers cluster at Level 1-2 (awareness of AI but no coherent strategy). Solcus brings them to Level 3-4 by giving them a knowledge infrastructure that makes AI adoption coherent.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Francis de Vericourt's challenge ([[Interview 020 — Francis de Vericourt (ESMT Professor)]]) — "what's missing in the middle?" — maps to the gap between Level 1 and Level 3. HAIC-MM names this as the transformation problem. Solcus is the bridge.
- **Product implication:** We could adapt the HAIC-MM maturity assessment as our onboarding diagnostic. "Where is your company on the knowledge maturity spectrum?" becomes our initial client scoping tool — free, positions us as experts, creates a natural entry point.

### 6. "AI Adoption Readiness Assessment" capability = potential Solcus entry product

One of the 32 capabilities is specifically "Assess readiness for AI by evaluating infrastructure, skills, and available resources." The free tool at www.haicmm.com does this generically. Solcus could offer a knowledge-specific version.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** This validates Lorenz's suggestion ([[Interview 019 — Lorenz Essing (EMH Partners, Germany)]]) that we need a diagnostic step before the engagement. A "Knowledge Readiness Assessment" (free, 30-min, generates a report) could be our top-of-funnel tool — analogous to how HAIC-MM uses the assessment to drive adoption of the full framework.

### 7. 40% productivity gains = pitch deck stat

"Studies indicate that organizations employing human-AI collaboration can achieve up to 40% productivity gains." (Section 1 of the paper, citing academic literature.) This is cited in an INCOSE-published, peer-reviewed systems engineering journal.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Adds to our ROI stack. The Maciej/Translink case ([[Interview 023 — Maciej Bogacz (Holon Translink, Poland)]]) provided the 40-60x ROI frame; the Bauermeister case ([[Interview 014 — Ulrich Bauermeister (Filigran, Germany)]]) gave us the €20K/forgotten-experiment figure. HAIC-MM gives us academic backing for the productivity dimension.

### 8. GDPR / ethics dimension confirms Marco's friction

The "AI Ethics & Human Oversight" dimension includes "AI data compliance: Ensure AI operations comply with data protection laws and standards." This was one of the dimensions where initial focus group sentiment was most negative before refinement — indicating real practitioner concern.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Marco Nortmeier ([[Interview 022 — Marco Nortmeier (Filigran, Germany)]]) raised GDPR and internal data governance as barriers to a self-built system. This is not a Filigran-specific concern — it's a documented industry-wide friction point for SMEs. Our methodology must explicitly address data ownership, residency, and access controls in the PoC design.

### 9. The "human-centered methodology" moat is academically validated

HAIC-MM distinguishes itself from existing AI maturity models by being "human-centered" rather than purely technical. The paper found that existing models "overlooked the importance of collaborative human-AI interactions, employee upskilling, and fostering staff trust in AI outputs" — all of which HAIC-MM addresses explicitly.

- **Solcus cross-reference:** Identical moat argument. Our methodology is valuable precisely because it puts human knowledge extraction first, before any AI tooling. Marco ([[Interview 022]]) and Francis ([[Interview 020]]) independently confirmed: "methodology, not model" is the differentiation. HAIC-MM's existence as a validated framework confirms that the human-methodology angle is a legitimate and defensible product moat in the SME AI space.

## How HAIC-MM Differs From Solcus

| Dimension | HAIC-MM | Solcus |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Problem addressed | How to adopt AI effectively | What knowledge exists to make AI adoption valuable |
| Customer journey stage | Already considering AI → wants a roadmap | Before AI adoption → needs knowledge infrastructure |
| Data source | Survey-based self-assessment | Interview-based expert extraction |
| Output | Maturity score + strategic roadmap | Queryable knowledge base + succession-ready documentation |
| Business model | Free online tool (academic/nonprofit) | €15-25K service engagement → SaaS |
| Moat | Academic validation + brand | Trust + methodology + SME-domain expertise |

**Key relationship:** HAIC-MM's "AI Adoption Readiness Assessment" is the gateway to what HAIC-MM prescribes. Solcus is what fills the gap HAIC-MM identifies — you can't adopt AI effectively if your organizational knowledge is locked in people's heads. HAIC-MM diagnoses the problem; Solcus solves the precondition.

## What We Can Learn From HAIC-MM

1. **Use the 5 maturity levels as ICP segmentation** — target Level 1-2 companies (exploration/enabled) who are ready to move up but lack the knowledge infrastructure to do so.
2. **Free diagnostic as top-of-funnel** — a "Knowledge Readiness Assessment" mirrors HAIC-MM's assessment tool. Free, creates engagement, generates a report that makes our value obvious.
3. **GDPR compliance must be front-loaded** — the ethics dimension was the hardest to validate with practitioners. Make data governance a first-class feature, not an afterthought.
4. **40% productivity stat is citable** — use in pitch deck with INCOSE attribution.
5. **Psychological safety framing** — position our extraction methodology as creating psychological safety (campfire conversations), not surveillance or knowledge extraction under pressure.
