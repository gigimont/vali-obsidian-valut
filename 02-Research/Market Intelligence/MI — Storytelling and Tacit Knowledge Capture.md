## What This Is

Three practitioner and academic sources on converting tacit (experiential) knowledge into explicit, sharable form. Bloomfire provides statistics on how widespread the problem is. KMI Blog explains WHY storytelling works neurologically. Glasgow Caledonian provides the SECI theoretical framework and makes the case for storyboarding as a structured capture method.

---

## Key Insights Relevant to Solcus

### 1. The scale of the problem: 73% of employees rely on undocumented knowledge

Bloomfire's 2025 Value Report: 73% of employees say they frequently rely on coworker knowledge that isn't documented anywhere. Fewer than 1 in 4 companies have a system to capture it.

**Solcus angle:** This is the precise gap we solve. The 73% figure is a deployable stat in pitch decks and investor conversations. It frames the problem as systemic, not a founder personality quirk.

### 2. Tacit knowledge has two dimensions (Nonaka SECI model)

Per Lee & Egmu (Glasgow Caledonian), drawing on Nonaka & Takeuchi:
- **Technical tacit:** know-how, skills, physical or procedural expertise (e.g. how the founder reads a client in the first meeting)
- **Cognitive tacit:** beliefs, values, mental models, worldview (e.g. what counts as a "trustworthy" partner)

The SECI cycle — Socialisation → Externalisation → Combination → Internalisation — describes how knowledge moves between tacit and explicit. **Externalisation** (tacit → explicit) is where Solcus operates.

**Solcus angle:** Founders hold BOTH types. The technical tacit is easier to extract (process, procedure, decision rule). The cognitive tacit is harder — it requires narrative elicitation, not structured questioning. This is why campfire storytelling works and a checklist doesn't.

### 3. Brains are wired for stories

"Our brains seem to be wired to easily and almost automatically organize information into stories" (Reamy, 2002, cited in KMI Blog). Storytelling captures what checklists miss: background, environment, and emotions associated with a specific situation — the nuances and subtleties that make tacit knowledge non-transferable through documentation.

**Solcus angle:** Scientific backing for the campfire method (Pattern 28). Not just a nice workshop technique — it matches the cognitive architecture of how humans store and retrieve knowledge.

### 4. Storytelling mechanisms for knowledge transfer

KMI Blog identifies four transfer mechanisms:
- **Contextualization:** Stories capture the "why" of a decision, not just the "what"
- **Ideation:** Collaborative storytelling generates new ideas at the intersection of experiences
- **Transfer of wisdom:** Anecdotes, case studies, and examples illustrate judgment under uncertainty
- **Cultural transmission:** Unwritten rules and values travel through narrative, not policy documents

**Solcus angle:** Our capture session isn't just extraction — it's also cultural transmission in real-time. The successor who watches the founder tell the story of a near-failure receives the judgment framework, not just the outcome.

### 5. Structured methods for capture

Bloomfire recommends:
- Recording customer service calls (tone and inflection = impossible to document in manuals)
- After-action reviews with wins AND losses
- Structured knowledge sharing sessions (record → archive → make searchable)

Glasgow Caledonian recommends storyboarding: presenting scenarios and having the expert walk through their reasoning step-by-step, creating a visual/structured narrative that can be reviewed and amended.

**Solcus angle:** Storyboarding maps closely to how we should structure our Phase 1 engagement. Session 1 = campfire (unstructured narrative). Session 2 = storyboard (structured walk-through of key decision moments with the founder annotating their reasoning). Session 3 = validation (successor tests the extracted rules).

### 6. Preventing knowledge loss at succession

Bloomfire frames the urgency: "capturing and sharing tacit knowledge is crucial... preventing any knowledge loss when employees leave." The turnover framing is directly analogous to succession: the knowledge doesn't just leave — it was never made visible in the first place.

**Solcus angle:** The pitch isn't "let's document your procedures." It's "let's make visible what has never been visible — before it disappears."

## What We Can Learn

1. **Stat to use:** 73% of employees rely on undocumented coworker knowledge (Bloomfire 2025 Value Report)
2. **SECI as methodology backbone:** We operate in the Externalisation quadrant. We can use this framework to explain what we do academically without jargon
3. **Two-session structure:** Campfire (unstructured) → Storyboarding (structured) mirrors the natural and contrived technique sequence in KE literature
4. **Cognitive tacit is the hard part:** Technical tacit can be extracted by a structured interview. Cognitive tacit (the judgment, the values) requires storytelling and narrative elicitation — this is our differentiation from generic "knowledge management" tools
