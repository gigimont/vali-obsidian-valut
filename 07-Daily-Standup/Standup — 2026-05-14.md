- **Name chosen: Solcus.** From Latin "sulcus" (brain fold where knowledge is stored) + Italian "solco" (furrow — the trace left by decades of work). Captures both the second-brain concept and the organic, earthy SME context.
- **Proof of concept roadmap agreed:** Filigran (family business, Obsidian approach) → AWW (Hubi + AI partner, hands-on collaboration) → Bergerhoff/FDU (third-party validation) → eventually Max Stein/BSV steel merger (scale play). Each stage builds on the previous.
- **Competitive advantage confirmed: methodology, not technology.** Francis ([[Interview 020 — Francis de Vericourt (ESMT Professor)]]) and the week's conversations crystallised this. The moat is HOW we extract knowledge (campfire storytelling, prompting protocol, domain expertise), not WHAT tools we use. Any consulting firm could deploy the same LLMs — they can't get the trust.
- **Governance structure: Filigran as co-development partner, NOT governance relationship.** Legal entity separation is critical for investor confidence. Filigran provides the PoC environment and potential LOI for credibility. No equity entanglement, no payroll dependency. Clean separation between Solcus and the family business.
- **ICP dual structure clarified:** The PAYING customer (ICP) is the company — manufacturing, 20-100+ employees, basic digitalization. The USE CASE targets the motivated technical insider (Planungsleiter, senior engineer). Company pays, persona uses.
- **Three operational pillars for the business:** (1) Outreach and network maintenance (~1/3 of time), (2) Obsidian vault maintenance (~1/3), (3) Flexible development — product, pitch, applications (~1/3).

## Key Insights This Week

### Product as a service, not a software product
The week confirmed: we are a specialised consulting practice with proprietary AI-enabled methodology. The AI is the tool, not the product. The methodology (campfire storytelling + domain expertise + trust) is the product. Francis said it directly: "You're a consulting company. Your competitive advantage is access, not technology."

### The transformation methodology gap (Francis's challenge)
Between "we collect data through three pillars" and "we deliver a second brain," there is an unarticulated transformation step. HOW does raw interview data + screen recordings + document scans become a queryable knowledge base? This is the "missing middle" that needs to be filled — and it's where the moat will be built. Life Book's interview technology may hold the answer.

### The "learn by doing" approach
Instead of planning a perfect methodology, start building an Obsidian brain for Filigran using the same approach as our own project vault. Strategy and methodology will crystallise from hands-on trial and error. "We are the proof of concept."

## Strategic Notes

### Governance principle
Filigran is a co-development partner, not an investor or employer. Any financial arrangement (LOIs, invoicing) must preserve legal entity separation. Solcus owns its methodology and IP independently. This is non-negotiable for future investor credibility.

### Three business rhythms
1. **Outreach & network** — the growth engine. Every contact opens 2-3 more. Maintenance is growing. Needs structure.
2. **Obsidian & documentation** — the knowledge engine. Our own second brain. Must stay current or the project loses its memory.
3. **Flexible development** — the creative space. Product methodology, pitch preparation, applications, proof of concept work. This is where progress happens.

Proportioning time across these three is a meta-skill we need to develop. It's becoming a real operational challenge.
