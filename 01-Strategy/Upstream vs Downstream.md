# Upstream vs Downstream

#strategy #unresolved #critical

> **Status:** UNRESOLVED — carry both hypotheses into fieldwork

---

## The Question

Do we serve founders **at the moment of succession** (downstream, age 65-75) or **10-15 years earlier** with a readiness product (upstream, age 50-60)?

## Downstream (succession moment)
- Founder is 65-75, actively thinking about stepping back
- Emotional, urgent, high-stakes
- **Problem:** founder is a terrible customer (no urgency until crisis, emotional, resists paying)
- **Sales cycle:** long, unpredictable, often stalls

## Upstream (readiness product)
- Founder is 50-60, business is thriving
- Sold as "build a business that runs without you" (appealing for reasons beyond succession)
- **Better economics:** clearer ROI, shorter sales cycle, less emotional resistance
- **Creates own pipeline:** succession-ready businesses 5-10 years later
- **Problem:** no felt urgency. "Why fix what isn't broken?"

## What First Interview Suggests

[[Interview 001 — Successor]] found a **thriving business with no crisis** where the successor sees the trap. This is the upstream scenario — the problem exists years before handover but nobody frames it as a succession issue yet.

## Resolution Method

**Do not resolve by argument. Resolve by data.** Interview both populations:
- Founders ready to sell/hand over now (downstream)
- Founders 5-10 years from needing to, with successors entering the business (upstream)

Compare: pain level, willingness-to-pay, competitive landscape.

## Update — May 12, 2026 (post-Interviews 001–019)

The binary framing (upstream vs downstream) may be insufficient. Three positions now have evidence:

**Upstream (before crisis):** Preferred. Founders like Stefan Weiler ([[Interview 012 — Stefan Weiler (Filigran, Germany)]]) and David ([[Interview 013 — David (Textile Manufacturing, Germany)]]) confirm: if knowledge had been captured early, the transition would have been smoother. Pre-transformation documentation ([[Interview 016 — Stefan Bergerhoff (BWB-Gruppe, Germany)]], [[Interview 018 — Hubertus von Huelsen (AWW, Germany)]]) is an upstream application that doesn't require a succession trigger at all.

**Downstream (during crisis):** Sometimes unavoidable. Vetreria Rachello ([[Interview 007 — Vetreria Rachello (Italy)]]) shows what happens when there's no upstream preparation: value erodes, options narrow. M&A due diligence ([[Interview 008 — Antonio Rizza (M&A)]], [[Interview 017 — Armin Struckmeier (NUK Novatex, Germany)]], [[Interview 019 — Lorenz Essing (EMH Partners, Germany)]]) is a downstream application with clear budget and urgency.

**Always-on (continuous):** Lorenz Essing ([[Interview 019 — Lorenz Essing (EMH Partners, Germany)]]) challenged the binary: "The value of knowledge is event-driven. M&A is a huge event but it can happen at any time." This suggests a product that monitors knowledge concentration continuously, with escalation at event moments (departure, acquisition, restructuring).

**Current strategic direction:** Enter downstream (M&A, highest urgency and budget), retain always-on (recurring revenue from continuous monitoring), expand upstream (founder-side succession and pre-transformation documentation).
