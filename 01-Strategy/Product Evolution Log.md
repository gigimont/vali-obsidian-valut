# Product Evolution Log

#strategy #core #living-document

> **Purpose:** Chronological record of how the product thesis, ICP, and GTM strategy evolved through fieldwork. Each entry captures WHAT changed, WHY it changed, and WHAT triggered it.
> **Status:** Living document. Update after any strategic pivot or major reframe.
> **Last updated:** May 12, 2026

## Phase 0 — Pre-fieldwork (Weeks 1-2)

### Starting thesis
"Documenting operations in organically grown SMEs will free up mental capacity in founders and senior employees to focus on securing the company's future."

### Starting product concept
Go to the company, do interviews + document review + computer-use tracking + shadowing → AI-enabled tool maps, organizes, and documents everything → deliver a report/map of how the business runs.

### Starting ICP
Vague: "small and medium enterprises going through succession." No size range, no geography, no trigger defined.

### Key assumptions (untested)
- The founder is the customer
- "Succession" is one problem
- Documentation is the deliverable
- AI can capture tacit knowledge (feasibility unknown)

---

## Phase 1 — Problem validation (Weeks 2-3, Interviews 001-004)

### Pivot 1: Succession is four sub-problems, not one
- **Trigger:** LLM Council pressure-test + Interview 001 (Successor)
- **What changed:** "Succession" decomposed into: matching, readiness, emotional, deal-structuring. We chose to focus on readiness.
- **What was abandoned:** The idea that succession is a single solvable problem.

### Pivot 2: The customer is the successor, not the founder
- **Trigger:** Interview 001 (Successor)
- **What changed:** The person who FEELS the pain most acutely is the one stepping into the role, not the one stepping out. The successor sees the knowledge gap; the founder doesn't feel it because the knowledge is in their own head.
- **What was abandoned:** Founder-as-primary-buyer assumption (partially — founders remain a secondary buyer).

### Pivot 3: "Digital due diligence" as a framing
- **Trigger:** Interview 004 (Prisma Founder, Romania)
- **What changed:** Prisma coined "digital due diligence" as a label for what we do. Also introduced: parallel delivery (Pattern 7), internal champion requirement (Pattern 6), neutral mirror framing (Pattern 8).
- **What survived:** Parallel delivery and neutral mirror became permanent design principles.

## Phase 2 — ICP sharpening (Weeks 3-4, Interviews 005-011)

### Pivot 4: From "documentation" to "decision replication"
- **Trigger:** Interview 003 (Merih, Finance Professor) — introduced Clonable (US startup doing decision replication)
- **What changed:** The product shifted from "document processes" to "capture how the founder decides." This was aspirational but influenced the direction.
- **What was abandoned (later):** "Decision replication" as a label — replaced by "judgment framework capture" after Interview 011.

### Pivot 5: M&A as a GTM angle
- **Trigger:** Interview 008 (Antonio Rizza, M&A professional)
- **What changed:** Documentation completeness maps directly to deal price (100-item checklist). Our product has immediate, quantifiable value for M&A buyers. This opened the buyer-side GTM path.
- **What survived:** M&A remains one of three GTM angles in v5 thesis.

### Pivot 6: "Judgment framework capture" replaces "decision replication"
- **Trigger:** Interview 011 (Dairy Chemicals Commercial, Italy)
- **What changed:** "Io ti do le chiavi di lettura, dopo sei tu che leggi lo spartito" — I give you the keys to reading, then you read the score. The product captures interpretive frameworks, not decisions themselves. This addresses Newton's feasibility objection (Interview 010).
- **What was abandoned:** The word "replication" — too ambitious, too threatening, and technically contested.

### Pivot 7: ICP defined — €5-25M revenue, 20-100 employees
- **Trigger:** Accumulation across Interviews 001-011
- **What changed:** ICP crystallized: family-owned, Germany/Italy, founder 55-72, basic digital maturity, approaching a transition. Too small = can't pay (Interview 011). Too large/digital = no wedge (Interview 006, Lynka).
- **What survived:** Core ICP holds, but "role-based vs size-based" question emerged in Phase 3.

## Phase 3 — Product crystallization (Weeks 4-5, Interviews 012-019)

### Pivot 8: The campfire method — stories beat processes
- **Trigger:** Interview 013 (Kevin, Textile Manufacturing, Germany)
- **What changed:** "Make it like a campfire. People tell their stories. They hate documenting processes but they love telling stories." This became the primary extraction methodology. Confirmed independently by Joerg (Interview 015) and Armin (Interview 017).
- **What was abandoned:** "Structured interviews" as the primary extraction method. Stories replace questionnaires.
- **Product design principle added:** "Tech must not feel like tech" (Pattern 29).

### Pivot 9: M&A-first GTM strategy
- **Trigger:** Product brainstorming session + accumulation of M&A validation (Interviews 008, 010, 017)
- **What changed:** Decision to sell to M&A buyers first (highest urgency, clearest budget, 4-6 week engagement). Founder-side product comes later.
- **Status:** Partially challenged by Lorenz (Interview 019) — see Pivot 12.

### Pivot 10: Three-pillar extraction architecture
- **Trigger:** Week 5 consolidation
- **What changed:** Extraction crystallized into three pillars: (1) conversational methodology (campfire), (2) computer-use tracking, (3) document/archive ingestion. Pillar 1 is the most uncertain and the likely moat.
- **What survived:** Architecture holds. Life Book (Interview 017) is the professional benchmark for Pillar 1.

### Pivot 11: Standardgrundlage-first sequencing for technical domains
- **Trigger:** Interview 016 (Stefan Bergerhoff, BWB-Gruppe) + Interview 018 (Hubi, AWW)
- **What changed:** For technical-domain SMEs, the normative document layer must be ingested BEFORE storytelling adds value. The campfire method is layer 2, not layer 1, in engineering contexts. Two independent sources confirmed this.
- **Product implication:** The methodology is not one-size-fits-all. Technical domains need a different entry sequence than relational/leadership domains.

### Pivot 12: "Always-on" challenges M&A-only GTM
- **Trigger:** Interview 019 (Lorenz Essing, EMH Partners)
- **What changed:** "The value of knowledge is event-driven. M&A is a huge event but it can happen at any time. I'm struggling to imagine why you'd limit to M&A." This suggests: enter via M&A (highest urgency), retain via always-on monitoring (recurring revenue).
- **Status:** Unresolved. Discussed but no decision made. Potentially the difference between a consulting practice and a SaaS business.

### Pivot 13: Three GTM angles (up from one)
- **Trigger:** Interviews 016, 017, 018, 019
- **What changed:** The product now has three validated applications:
  1. **Preserve & Scale** — founder succession, operational continuity, expert knowledge scaling
  2. **Acquire & Exit** — M&A documentation during DD + exit preparation before resale
  3. **Pre-transformation documentation** — "do your homework before McKinsey arrives" (Bergerhoff + Hubi)
- **Status:** All three validated by at least two independent sources. Pitch deck updated to show as "one product, two applications" (Preserve & Scale + Acquire & Exit). Pre-transformation may become a third.

### Pivot 14: Role-based vs size-based ICP
- **Trigger:** Interviews 016 (Bergerhoff, BWB-Gruppe) + 018 (Hubi, AWW)
- **What changed:** Both companies are ABOVE the ICP on size (600+ employees, 170M revenue) but match PERFECTLY on pain profile. The "motivated technical insider" (Planungsleiter, tool-making expert) is the same persona across company sizes. Question: should ICP be defined by company size or by role profile?
- **Status:** Unresolved. Added to ICP Definition v2 as an open question.

### Key concept: the "olive tree" framework
- **Trigger:** Interview 017 (Armin Struckmeier, NUK Novatex)
- **What it is:** You can't replace an organically grown leader 1:1. You cut the role into pieces — production company + sales company — and find someone for each piece.
- **Product implication:** "Olive tree mapping" could be an explicit deliverable: identify which branches of expertise exist, which live in one person's head, and help plan the split.

### Key lead: Life Book
- **Trigger:** Interview 017 (Armin Struckmeier)
- **What it is:** A YPO company that professionally interviews people and creates life stories. Now using AI. Armin's sister-in-law Andrea manages it.
- **Status:** Contact offered, not yet reached. This is either a competitor, a partner, or a methodology source. Highest-priority outreach.

## Current State (May 12, 2026)

**Product:** A 4-6 week service that captures context, culture, and decision logic from SME experts through three pillars (storytelling, computer tracking, document ingestion) and delivers a structured report + queryable knowledge system.

**ICP:** Family-owned SMEs, 20-100 employees, €5-25M revenue, Germany/Italy. BUT: the "motivated technical insider" persona may generalize across company sizes.

**GTM:** M&A entry (highest urgency) → always-on retention (recurring revenue) → upstream expansion (founder-side succession, pre-transformation).

**Open strategic questions:**
1. Always-on vs event-driven — consulting practice or SaaS?
2. Role-based vs size-based ICP — who exactly is the customer?
3. Can we actually extract tacit knowledge at sufficient quality? (Life Book may answer)
4. Who is the technical co-founder?
