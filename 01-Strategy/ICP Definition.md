# ICP Definition

> **Status:** v1 — May 2026. Based on Interviews 001–011.
> **Owner:** Giuseppe + Wolf

---

## Who This Is For

### Primary ICP (paying customer)
- **Company size:** 20–100 employees
- **Revenue:** €5–25M
- **Geography:** Germany or Italy (initial); broader Europe later
- **Sector:** Any organically grown SME (manufacturing, services, trade — not pure tech)
- **Founder situation:** Founder aged 55–72, approaching retirement OR has a 3–5 year sale horizon
- **Successor situation:** A successor exists — either internal (family member or long-tenured employee) who is afraid to take over, OR the company is preparing for external sale
- **Digital maturity:** Has a gestionale or basic ERP; uses WhatsApp/email operationally; has NOT yet invested in sophisticated digital tools. Knows AI exists but feels "under-advised."
- **Attitude:** Founder is open-minded enough to see the company as something to preserve, not just a salary. Values reputation and relationships.

### Secondary ICP (buyer-side)
- **Search fund GPs** (like Newton Campos): funds investing in young CEOs acquiring family businesses in the €10–25M EV range. Would benefit from pre-packaged operational documentation reducing the 1-year handover period.
- **Corporate M&A teams** (like Antonio Rizza): deal teams doing operational due diligence who face a 100-item checklist and would pay for pre-documented companies.

---

## Who This Is NOT For

### Too small
- Solo entrepreneurs or micro-businesses (1–5 employees, <€2M revenue) — our product costs more than their entire digitalization budget. They validate the thesis but are not paying customers.
- *Canonical example: [[Interview 011 — Dairy Chemicals Commercial (Italy)]] — sole trader, single employee, valid problem, wrong size.*

### Too digitally mature
- Companies that have already invested heavily in ERP, CRM, process tools. Our extraction methodology adds little to what they already have.
- *Canonical example: [[Interview 006 - John Lynch (Lynka)]] — €40-50M, 4th ERP system, CFO hired, competitor (Omnivisor) already engaged. No clean wedge.*

### No succession horizon
- Founders who explicitly intend to run the company for 15+ more years with no transition thoughts. No urgency = no purchase trigger.

### Founder-hostile
- Founders who see the business as a personal black box and refuse to let anyone inside (not even a family member). These exist but are rare and not worth pursuing.

---

## The Trigger

The ICP becomes a paying customer when ONE of these is present:
1. A successor has entered the business and is visibly struggling ("she's afraid of making decisions")
2. The company is 3–5 years from a planned sale and the founder knows documentation increases the sale price
3. A near-succession event has happened (father died, senior employee left) and the knowledge gap became visible overnight
