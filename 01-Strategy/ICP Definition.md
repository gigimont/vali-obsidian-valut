## v3 Update — May 20, 2026

### The wedge definition is now precise (Pattern 22 + Pattern 42)

The strongest cross-industry pattern in the dataset (Pattern 42, confirmed across construction, aluminium, eyewear, and engineering) combined with the ERP-ceiling reality (Pattern 22) defines the ICP wedge with new precision:

> **The Solco ICP is the head-of-department role layer (Planungsleiter, head of logistics, head of purchasing/product planning, technical feasibility bridge) inside Mittelstand companies that have hit the ERP ceiling, across any industry.**

Role profile matters more than industry. Size matters less than role.

### New ICP timing axis (Pattern 41)

The v2 ICP captured WHO and WHERE. v3 adds WHEN: the optimal engagement window is **2-5 years before key expert retirement**. Earlier engagement hits self-preservation hoarding (Pattern 25); later engagement loses extractable knowledge to attrition. Screen for retirement timing in outreach. ([[Interview 024 — Eberhard Müller-Menrad (Eyewear Mittelstand, Germany)]])

### New persona — Post-handover former CEO (champion, not customer)

Roland Wübbe ([[Interview 021 — Roland Wübbe (H&W Tiefbau, Germany)]]) and Eberhard Müller-Menrad (Interview 024) define a new role profile: former CEOs 1-3 years past their own succession or business exit. They are:

- Not paying customers (no operational authority)
- Over-represented in family business networks (Peter May / Hamburg Family Summit, YPO, YNG)
- Reflective — they have personally lived knowledge loss in retrospect
- High-value advocates and referrers
- A reliable source of warm introductions to their peers (current CEOs)

**Action:** Add "post-handover former CEO" as a research-interview ICP segment with the explicit goal of network-gateway access, not direct sales.

### New persona — Internal IT lead with operational authority

Marco Nortmeier ([[Interview 022 — Marco Nortmeier (Filigran, Germany)]]) defines a key enabler persona INSIDE the ICP company: the internal IT lead with authority to onboard new systems, navigate GDPR, and methodology-align with our approach. Not a separate ICP — but the Pattern 35 (three trust thresholds) operational-layer counterpart to the domain technical expert. Filigran's complete Pattern 35 alignment (Stefan Weiler at strategic, Bauermeister at technical, Marco at operational/IT) is the first fully-aligned case in the dataset.

**Action:** When pursuing a Mittelstand prospect, identify the equivalent of "Marco" — the operational IT lead — before pricing or scoping. This is the implementation enabler.

### Fourth industry confirmation (eyewear / Mittelstand consumer)

Eberhard Müller-Menrad's post-insolvency Mittelstand eyewear case (Interview 024) adds a fourth industry to Pattern 42's cross-industry validation:

- **Construction/precast:** Filigran (Bauermeister), BWB (Bergerhoff)
- **Aluminium manufacturing:** AWW (Hubi)
- **Textile manufacturing:** Familie David's textile firm (Interview 013)
- **Eyewear:** Müller-Menrad (Interview 024)

This is now industry-agnostic for any Mittelstand SME with an organically-grown structure. The next ICP-validation test is creative/fashion-driven industries where the bottleneck is trend intuition rather than process knowledge — flagged as a future expansion direction.

### Strategic question resolved: role-based, not size-based

v2 left open the question of whether the ICP should be size-based (20-100 employees, €5-25M revenue) or role-based (motivated technical insider with documentation pain). v3 resolves this: **role-based, with size as a secondary screen.**

The companies above the v2 size range (BWB-Gruppe ~600 employees, AWW ~170M revenue, Filigran ~110 employees) all have the same head-of-department bottleneck. The pain profile is independent of size. The v2 size range remains useful as a "where the math works for a 4-6 week engagement at €15-25K" filter, but it is not the ICP definition itself.

### MI-validated market scale ([[MI — Silver Tsunami and AI Knowledge Capture]])

- 43% of Germany's workforce retires by 2036 (Kempten University data)
- 57% of institutional knowledge in industrial sectors at risk in the next decade (McKinsey)
- 68% of industrial companies have no formal knowledge transfer program (ASTD)
- 40% of US manufacturing workforce eligible to retire within 10 years (BLS)

The macroeconomic context makes the Pattern 41 timing window not a marginal optimisation but a structural opportunity.

### Primary ICP (paying customer)
- **Company size:** 20–100 employees
- **Revenue:** €5–25M
- **Geography:** Germany or Italy (initial); broader Europe later
- **Sector:** Any organically grown SME (manufacturing, services, trade — not pure tech)
- **Founder situation:** Founder aged 55–72, approaching retirement OR has a 3–5 year sale horizon
- **Successor situation:** A successor exists — either internal (family member or long-tenured employee) who is afraid to take over, OR the company is preparing for external sale
- **Digital maturity:** Has a gestionale or basic ERP; uses WhatsApp/email operationally; has NOT yet invested in sophisticated digital tools. Knows AI exists but feels "under-advised."
- **Attitude:** Founder is open-minded enough to see the company as something to preserve, not just a salary. Values reputation and relationships.

### Emerging persona: the motivated technical insider
A distinct buyer profile has emerged from Interviews 014, 016, and 018: technically capable insiders (Planungsleiter, tool-making experts, operations leads) who see the knowledge bottleneck daily, have internal credibility to propose solutions, but fail at the last 10% of implementation. They are NOT founders. They are NOT successors. They are operational experts who want the tool for themselves.

Key characteristics:
- Role: technical lead, planning manager, senior engineer
- Pain: they ARE the bottleneck and they know it ("wenn ich nicht da bin, fehlt irgendwas")
- Digital context: company may have ERP, but their knowledge sits outside it
- Motivation: free up their own time, reduce single-point-of-failure risk, enable AI implementation
- Trust threshold: HIGH openness at the technical layer; resistance only at the strategic/business-model layer

This persona may exist in companies ABOVE the current size-based ICP (AWW at 170M revenue, BWB-Gruppe at 600+ employees). The strategic question: **should the ICP be defined by company size or by role profile?** Both Bergerhoff and Hubi match the pain profile perfectly despite being in companies above the size wedge.

*Canonical examples: [[Interview 014 — Ulrich Bauermeister (Filigran, Germany)]], [[Interview 016 — Stefan Bergerhoff (BWB-Gruppe, Germany)]], [[Interview 018 — Hubertus von Huelsen (AWW, Germany)]]*

### Secondary ICP (buyer-side)
- **Search fund GPs** (like Newton Campos): funds investing in young CEOs acquiring family businesses in the €10–25M EV range. Would benefit from pre-packaged operational documentation reducing the 1-year handover period.
- **Corporate M&A teams** (like Antonio Rizza): deal teams doing operational due diligence who face a 100-item checklist and would pay for pre-documented companies.
- **Growth equity / PE board members** (like Lorenz Essing at EMH): board-level investors who need "information vulnerability mapping" as an ongoing governance tool — not just during DD but continuously. Would pay for always-on knowledge concentration monitoring across portfolio companies. (Source: [[Interview 019 — Lorenz Essing (EMH Partners, Germany)]])
- **Add-on acquisition teams**: EMH's smaller add-on deals (€5-20M) have LESS formal DD than headline deals — exactly where our product fills the gap. (Source: [[Interview 019 — Lorenz Essing (EMH Partners, Germany)]])

## Who This Is NOT For

### Too small
- Solo entrepreneurs or micro-businesses (1–5 employees, <€2M revenue) — our product costs more than their entire digitalization budget. They validate the thesis but are not paying customers.
- *Canonical example: [[Interview 011 — Dairy Chemicals Commercial (Italy)]] — sole trader, single employee, valid problem, wrong size.*

### Too digitally mature
- Companies that have already invested heavily in ERP, CRM, process tools. Our extraction methodology adds little to what they already have.
- *Canonical example: [[Interview 006 - John Lynch (Lynka)]] — €40-50M, 4th ERP system, CFO hired, competitor (Omnivisor) already engaged. No clean wedge.*

### No succession horizon
- Founders who explicitly intend to run the company for 15+ more years with no transition thoughts. No urgency = no purchase trigger.

### Founder-hostile
- Founders who see the business as a personal black box and refuse to let anyone inside (not even a family member). These exist but are rare and not worth pursuing.

## The Trigger

The ICP becomes a paying customer when ONE of these is present:
1. A successor has entered the business and is visibly struggling ("she's afraid of making decisions")
2. The company is 3–5 years from a planned sale and the founder knows documentation increases the sale price
3. A near-succession event has happened (father died, senior employee left) and the knowledge gap became visible overnight
