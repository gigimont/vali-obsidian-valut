The Tacit Knowledge Conundrum and How AI Is Learning to Overcome It

As artificial intelligence grows more advanced, a persistent question lingers: Why can’t AI fully replace many human jobs, even those that seem routine? The answer lies in a subtle but powerful challenge known as the tacit knowledge problem, sometimes called the articulation barrier.

Tacit knowledge includes the skills, intuition, and know-how that people pick up through experience but find difficult to put into words or instructions. For instance, you can explain the basics of riding a bike (balance, pedaling, steering) but actually doing it requires hands-on practice, muscle memory, and real-time feedback. These are things learned by doing, not just by listening. In the same way, a seasoned technician might sense that a machine is about to fail, or a nurse might notice the first signs of a patient’s decline. These insights are rarely documented, yet they are critical to real-world performance.

This creates a conundrum for AI. Machine learning and automation systems excel at handling explicit data and following clear instructions. But if workers cannot fully explain the details of their work, it becomes nearly impossible to train AI to take over. As a result, jobs that rely heavily on tacit knowledge, creativity, or nuanced judgment are still largely resistant to automation

Bridging the Gap: Observational Learning at Work

To tackle the challenge of tacit knowledge, a new strategy is emerging: observational learning through video and workflow capture. Rather than asking experts to explain every detail, this approach enables AI systems to learn by watching people do their jobs in real-world settings.

Subject matter experts record themselves performing real tasks, narrating their decisions, demonstrating each step, and solving problems as they naturally occur. These recordings are then processed by an automated AI pipeline that: - Transcribes and analyzes the videos - Extracts workflows, step-by-step actions, and key decision points - Converts these into structured, actionable run-books and knowledge artifacts - Indexes them for easy retrieval and ongoing learning

This method is similar to how humans learn through apprenticeship. Instead of relying on instruction manuals, the AI “watches” and begins to imitate expert behavior. It captures not just what is done, but also why it is done, both the visible steps and the expert’s reasoning.

Over time, tacit knowledge becomes visible and transferable. The AI builds a working model of expert intuition, which continues to evolve through feedback from human reviewers. The result is not just automation, but true augmentation, AI that works alongside people, continuously learning from their expertise.
