	A few takeaways from our 2025 German Tech Ecosystem Report (⬇️link to full report in comments)  
	  
	Funding hasn't disappeared, but the "spray and pray" era is clearly over. Capital is stabilizing, yet increasingly concentrated in a few strategic sectors (AI, Health, resilience tech).  
	Berlin is still the main hub, but Munich is gaining real momentum. M&A is (very) slowly waking up again. And the same structural question remains: everyone loves Series A... but who loves Series C??

	• How VC funding dynamics in Germany are shifting

	• The startups that could drive the next unicorn wave  
	• The evolution of the German capital stack  
	• What exit activity might look like going into 2026

		[𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝟱 𝗺𝗲𝘁𝗿𝗶𝗰𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗺𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝗮𝘁 𝗦𝗲𝗲𝗱 (𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗺𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗸𝗶𝗹𝗹 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗳𝘂𝗻𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗿𝗼𝘂𝗻𝗱)  
	  
	After reviewing dozens of Seed pitches at XAnge, I keep seeing the same pattern: Founders nail the vision. But fall apart on the numbers.  
	"We hit €40k MRR" – sounds great. Until we dig deeper and it turns out a big chunk is from trials and one-time fees, not recurring payments.  
	  
	The reality: 𝟰𝟲% 𝗼𝗳 𝗩𝗖𝘀 𝗻𝗼𝘄 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝗿𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗻𝘂𝗲 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗻 𝗮𝘁 𝗽𝗿𝗲-𝗦𝗲𝗲𝗱. For Seed rounds? €150–500k ARR is the new baseline.  
	  
	Here are the 5 metrics that matter – and how NOT to track them:  
	𝟭. 𝗠𝗥𝗥/𝗔𝗥𝗥 (𝗯𝘂𝘁 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝗶𝘁 𝗵𝗼𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘁)  
	❌ Annual contracts ÷ 12  
	❌ Pilots counted as recurring revenue  
	✅ Only actual, recurring payments  
	  
	𝟮. 𝗠𝗼𝗻𝘁𝗵𝗹𝘆 𝗚𝗿𝗼𝘄𝘁𝗵 𝗥𝗮𝘁𝗲 Pro tip: Track absolute numbers ("added 4 customers") when you're under 50 customers. "33% growth" means nothing when your base is 12.  
	  
	𝟯. 𝗚𝗿𝗼𝘀𝘀 𝗠𝗮𝗿𝗴𝗶𝗻 At Seed, a clear path from 40% to 70% beats stagnating at 55%. Series A target: 70–80% → Trajectory matters more than your current number  
	  
	𝟰. 𝗖𝗔𝗖 𝗣𝗮𝘆𝗯𝗮𝗰𝗸 Seed reality: 12–24 months (often longer). The key: showing improvement and understanding the relationship between acquisition cost and customer value.  
	  
	𝟱. 𝗕𝘂𝗿𝗻 𝗠𝘂𝗹𝘁𝗶𝗽𝗹𝗲 Seed: 2–5x is normal (€2–5 burned per €1 new ARR). Series A: <2x expected  
	  
	𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝗶𝗴𝗴𝗲𝘀𝘁 𝗺𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝗜 𝘀𝗲𝗲: Founders feeling pressure to inflate their numbers. Here's the truth: investors prefer honest metrics with real improvement over "perfect" numbers that don't add up. Authenticity wins.  
	  
	𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗶𝗻𝘃𝗲𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗿𝘀 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝘄𝗮𝗻𝘁 𝘁𝗼 𝘀𝗲𝗲:  
	→ Measurement discipline (even with small numbers)  
	→ Understanding of unit economics  
	→ Improvement over time  
	→ Honest numbers
