		[An MVP is now a weekend project. A startup still isn't.  
	  
	Since 2 weeks, I'm working with 30+ future founders, operators and engineers as part of our 3-months ESMT incubation program SEP. Most arrived without a team or an idea. By the end of week one, both or one of the two had usually changed.  
	  
	In the past, I had to often push founders to launch, and not research an idea to death. But now that anyone can ship an MVP in a weekend and iteration cycles are getting shorter and shorter, the question of should we build this at all has gotten much harder, not easier. I'm observing that founders increasingly use AI not only to develop products, but also to do secondary research. To some extent it is useful, but most of the time it is dangerous and leads talents into the wrong direction.  
	  
	As](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7458409187098787840/) [**Steve Blank**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/steveblank/) described almost twenty years ago: a startup is "a temporary organization designed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model."  
	  
	AI accelerates this search. It does not replace it. If you want to build an extraordinary, generational company, you will rarely get there through secondary research or AI-generated MVPs. You get there by going out, doing primary research, finding patterns nobody else has seen, and forming unique beliefs (technical, commercial, or both) that you're willing to bet on. When iteration cycles are getting faster and faster, it is more important than ever to remind founders to take a step back and remind them of what customer discovery actually is.  
	  
	That's one of the core skill our program is built to train.
